\noindent\textbf{Applied Vacuum Engineering: Volume V} \\
This document translates the foundational mechanics of Variable Spacetime Impedance into the realm of biological chemistry.

\begin{abstract}
    Protein self-assembly is often framed as a computational problem (Levinthal's paradox), addressed by heuristic structure prediction or large-scale force-field simulation. 

    \textbf{Volume V: Topological Biology \& Molecular Circuitry} applies the Applied Vacuum Engineering (AVE) substrate model to molecular biology, mapping amino-acid sequences to topological impedance values and modelling organic molecules as structural AC transmission lines. 

    On this basis the volume develops secondary-structure motifs such as alpha-helices and beta-sheets as minimum-topological-strain configurations of the underlying $1/r^3$ vacuum lattice, without per-structure data fitting.
\end{abstract}
