\section{The Mathematical Topology of Mass}
Before analyzing specific particle geometries, the text formally defines the foundational energy and topological constraints of the continuum. In a continuous non-linear substrate, stable particles are defined as finite-energy soliton solutions to the generalised \textbf{Faddeev-Skyrme Energy Functional}:
\begin{resultbox}{Faddeev-Skyrme Energy Functional}
\begin{equation}
    E = \int \left( \frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu \vec{n} \cdot \partial^\mu \vec{n} + \frac{1}{4e^2}(\partial_\mu \vec{n} \times \partial_\nu \vec{n})^2 \right) d^3x
\end{equation}
\end{resultbox}
where $\vec{n}$ represents the normalised local LC displacement vector of the vacuum. The first term dictates the standard kinematic energy of the field, while the second non-linear term (scaled by the dielectric yield bound $e$) repels the strands, preventing the knot from collapsing into a singularity.

The specific topological identity of any particle is classified by its \textbf{Hopf Charge} or \textbf{Gauss Linking Number} ($Q$), an invariant topological integer defining the number of times the internal magnetic flux lines intertwine:
\begin{resultbox}{Gauss Linking Number (Hopf Charge)}
\begin{equation}
    Q = \frac{1}{16\pi^2} \int \epsilon_{ijk} \vec{n} \cdot (\partial_i \vec{n} \times \partial_j \vec{n}) \ d^3x
\end{equation}
\end{resultbox}
Because this topological index $Q$ is conserved under continuous domain deformations, it derives the conservation laws (e.g., Baryon Number and Lepton Number) from geometric invariants.
