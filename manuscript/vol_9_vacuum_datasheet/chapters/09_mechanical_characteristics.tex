\section{Substrate as Cosserat Micropolar Continuum}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_cosserat_continuum}

Per Axiom~1 (Ch.~\ref{ch:fundamental_axioms}, canonical equation file \kbleaf{common\_equations/eq\_axiom\_1.tex}; CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2), the natural vacuum is a 3D chiral Laves K4 \emph{Cosserat} crystal. Each K4 node carries six intrinsic degrees of freedom:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Three \textbf{translational} DOFs --- capacitive coupling $\varepsilon_0$, identified with the electric field $\mathbf{E}$.
    \item Three \textbf{microrotational} DOFs --- inductive coupling $\mu_0$, identified with the magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$. The Cosserat microrotational DOF \emph{is} the substrate-native origin of intrinsic spin.
\end{itemize}

This is the load-bearing structural distinction between the substrate and a Cauchy (classical-elastic) continuum: a Cauchy medium has translational DOFs only and recovers Lam\'e elasticity; a Cosserat micropolar medium adds independent micro-rotations $\omega$ at every material point, with bond couple-stress $\gamma_c$ coupling neighbouring rotations. The intrinsic-spin-from-microrotation interpretation is canonical (CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2 Axiom~1; Vol~1 Ch.~\ref{sec:cosserat_primer} Cosserat primer).

The substrate is therefore documented in the engineering datasheet as a Cosserat micropolar elastic medium with EE-substrate-native projection: translational DOFs $\leftrightarrow$ \textbf{capacitor} (dielectric storage); microrotational DOFs $\leftrightarrow$ \textbf{inductive flywheel} (rotational moment-of-inertia + magnetic flux storage); inter-node couple-stress $\gamma_c$ $\leftrightarrow$ \textbf{transformer mutual-inductance gradient} (canonical at \kbleaf{common/translation-tables/translation-circuit.md} \S 4 mapping rows).

\section{Vacuum Poisson Ratio $\nu_{vac} = 2/7$}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_poisson}

The Poisson ratio of the substrate at the cold-lattice limit is the substrate-axiomatic identity

\[
\nu_{vac} = \frac{3 K_{vac} - 2 G_{vac}}{2 (3 K_{vac} + G_{vac})} = \frac{6 G_{vac} - 2 G_{vac}}{2 (6 G_{vac} + G_{vac})} = \frac{4}{14} = \frac{2}{7}.
\]

This is \emph{not} a fitted parameter. It is forced by two independent axiomatic inputs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Axiom~1 LC + Axiom~2 packing identity: the Feng--Thorpe--Garboczi EMT quadratic at packing fraction $p^* = 8\pi\alpha$ has the unique trace-reversal solution $K/G = 2$.
    \item Standard isotropic elastic identity: $\nu = (3K - 2G)/[2(3K + G)]$ evaluated at $K = 2G$ gives $\nu = 2/7$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cosserat Couple-Stress and the Weak-Force Range}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_couple_stress}

The Cosserat couple-stress modulus $\gamma_c$ is the substrate-native bending inductance of the microrotational sector: the bond-level resistance to differential rotation between neighbouring K4 nodes. The substrate-native characteristic length built from the couple-stress / shear-modulus ratio is

\[
l_c = \sqrt{\gamma_c / G_{vac}},
\]

and is identified with the weak-force range $r_W \approx 10^{-18}$~m (canonical at \kbleaf{vol2/particle-physics/ch05-electroweak-mechanics/gauge-boson-masses.md} line~39; Vol~II's Electroweak Mechanics chapter).

\textbf{Substrate mechanism.} Per the canonical derivation (Vol~II's Electroweak Mechanics chapter; \kbleaf{gauge-boson-masses.md}): weak interactions lack the kinetic energy required to overcome the ambient LC rotational inductance. Any physical excitation operating \emph{below} the medium's natural cutoff frequency becomes an evanescent wave. The static field equation transforms from the Laplace equation $\nabla^2 \theta = 0$ to the massive Helmholtz equation $\nabla^2 \theta - \theta / l_c^2 = 0$, whose Green's function is the Yukawa potential $V_{weak}(r) \propto e^{-r/l_c}/r$. The $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ bosons are the evanescent cutoff modes of the substrate's bending and torsional sectors respectively, with mass ratio $m_W/m_Z = \sqrt{7}/3$ set by the same $\nu_{vac} = 2/7$ Poisson identity (\S\ref{sec:vol9_mech_poisson} above).

\textbf{EE-substrate-native projection.} The Cosserat couple-stress modulus $\gamma_c$ \emph{is} the \textbf{transformer leakage inductance characteristic length} at the bond scale. The W/Z mass scale is set by the transformer-leakage-inductance characteristic frequency. The microrotational sector mass-gap $m_\omega \sim 1$~MeV (canonical at \kbleaf{common/trampoline-framework.md} line~188) is the \textbf{ferrite-Curie-temperature analog} for the substrate's magnetic-mode thermal-freeze threshold.

The Vol~9 magnetic / microrotational characteristics of the substrate --- the Cosserat flywheel inductance, the rotation-sector mass-gap, $B_{snap}$, and the weak-force range as a spec entry --- are documented separately in Ch.~10 (Magnetic / Microrotational Characteristics). This chapter records $\gamma_c$ as a bulk mechanical primitive; Ch.~10 develops the consequences for the magnetic sector.

\section{Mechanical Wave Speeds}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_wave_speeds}

The transverse (shear) and dilatational mechanical wave speeds of the substrate at the cold-lattice limit are set by the bulk moduli and density. \textbf{Two distinct longitudinal speeds} must be separated --- the A1-dilatation bulk-modulus port-mode and the solid P-wave (\kbleaf{03a\_device\_circuit\_models.tex}:80):

\begin{align}
v_T(0) &= \sqrt{G_{vac} / \rho_{bulk}} = c_0, \nonumber \\
v_{bulk}(0) &= \sqrt{K_{vac} / \rho_{bulk}} = \sqrt{2}\, c_0, \nonumber \\
c_L(0) &= \sqrt{\frac{K_{vac} + \tfrac{4}{3}G_{vac}}{\rho_{bulk}}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{10}{3}}\, c_0 \approx 1.83\, c_0. \nonumber
\end{align}

The transverse speed equals $c_0$ \emph{by construction} --- photons are transverse excitations of the LC lattice, so $v_T = c_0$ identically (canonical-source \kbleaf{src/ave/core/constants.py:705} \texttt{G\_VAC}; \kbleaf{src/ave/core/constants.py:697} \texttt{RHO\_BULK}). The \textbf{bulk-modulus dilatational speed} $v_{bulk} = \sqrt{2}\, c_0$ (the A1-scalar port-mode, which \emph{drops} the $4G/3$ shear term) follows from $K_{vac} = 2 G_{vac}$ (canonical-source \kbleaf{src/ave/core/constants.py:709} \texttt{V\_LONG}); the bulk-modulus dilatational quantity $v_{bulk}=\sqrt{2}\,c_0$ is the A1-scalar port-mode, \emph{not} the solid P-wave. The \textbf{solid longitudinal P-wave speed} $c_L = \sqrt{(K_{vac}+4G_{vac}/3)/\rho_{bulk}} = \sqrt{10/3}\, c_0$ (the standard isotropic-solid form at $\nu_{vac}=2/7$, which \emph{keeps} the $4G/3$ term) is the distinct second object; the two differ by whether the shear term is carried, both are $\alpha$-free, and the choice shifts only the bulk/shear gap location (canonical at \kbleaf{03a\_device\_circuit\_models.tex}:80).

\textbf{Saturation modulation.} At finite saturation operating point $A_0 \neq 0$, the substrate carries two distinct effective speeds, and conflating them is the canonical framework-leakage error (CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2):

\begin{itemize}
    \item $c_{EM}(A_0) = c_0 / S(A_0)$ --- the Maxwell phase velocity; appears in $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar c)$ and all Maxwell-equation derivations. Canonical at \kbleaf{vol1/claim-quality.md} (Symmetric vs Asymmetric Saturation).
    \item $c_{shear}(A_0) = c_0\, \sqrt{S(A_0)}$ --- the substrate mechanical / group / rest-mass velocity; tracks the Schwarzschild $c\sqrt{1 - r_s/r}$ reduction in the SYM-class weak-field gravity limit. This is the spec entry for the mechanical $v_T$ at operating point $A_0$. Canonical at \kbleaf{vol1/claim-quality.md} (Symmetric vs Asymmetric Saturation).
\end{itemize}

Both reduce to $c_0$ at the cold-lattice limit $S(0) = 1$. The mechanical-characteristics datasheet entry is $v_T(A_0) = c_{shear}(A_0) = c_0 \sqrt{S(A_0)}$. Under SYM-class saturation scaling (gravity-class realization: $\mu$ and $\varepsilon$ scale together so $Z_0$ stays invariant), this produces gravitational time dilation via the Schwarzschild reduction \emph{while} simultaneously preserving $\alpha$ exactly (canonical at \kbleaf{ave-kb/vol3/gravity/ch01-gravity-yield/alpha-invariance-symmetric-gravity.md}). The substrate-physics of the gravity-class SYM scaling is documented in Vol~3 Ch.~\ref{ch:gravity_and_yield}; this datasheet entry records $v_T(A_0) = c_{shear}(A_0)$ as the canonical mechanical wave-speed spec under saturation modulation.
