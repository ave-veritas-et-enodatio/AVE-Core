\section{Substrate as Cosserat Micropolar Continuum}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_cosserat_continuum}

Per Axiom~1 (Ch.~\ref{ch:fundamental_axioms}, canonical equation file \texttt{common\_equations/eq\_axiom\_1.tex}; CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2), the natural vacuum $\mathcal{M}_A$ is a 3D chiral Laves K4 \emph{Cosserat} crystal. Each K4 node carries six intrinsic degrees of freedom:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Three \textbf{translational} DOFs --- capacitive coupling $\varepsilon_0$, identified with the electric field $\mathbf{E}$.
    \item Three \textbf{microrotational} DOFs --- inductive coupling $\mu_0$, identified with the magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$. The Cosserat microrotational DOF \emph{is} the substrate-native origin of intrinsic spin.
\end{itemize}

This is the load-bearing structural distinction between $\mathcal{M}_A$ and a Cauchy (classical-elastic) continuum: a Cauchy medium has translational DOFs only and recovers Lam\'e elasticity; a Cosserat micropolar medium adds independent micro-rotations $\omega$ at every material point, with bond couple-stress $\gamma_c$ coupling neighbouring rotations. The intrinsic-spin-from-microrotation interpretation is canonical (CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2 Axiom~1; Vol~1 Ch.~\ref{sec:cosserat_primer} Cosserat primer).

The substrate is therefore documented in the engineering datasheet as a Cosserat micropolar elastic medium with EE-substrate-native projection: translational DOFs $\leftrightarrow$ \textbf{capacitor} (dielectric storage); microrotational DOFs $\leftrightarrow$ \textbf{inductive flywheel} (rotational moment-of-inertia + magnetic flux storage); inter-node couple-stress $\gamma_c$ $\leftrightarrow$ \textbf{transformer mutual-inductance gradient} (canonical at \texttt{common/translation-tables/translation-circuit.md} \S 4 mapping rows; \texttt{ave-ee-first-mapping} v1.0 means-test rows 10, 12).

\section{Vacuum Poisson Ratio $\nu_{vac} = 2/7$}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_poisson}

The Poisson ratio of $\mathcal{M}_A$ at the cold-lattice limit is the substrate-axiomatic identity

\[
\nu_{vac} = \frac{3 K_{vac} - 2 G_{vac}}{2 (3 K_{vac} + G_{vac})} = \frac{6 G_{vac} - 2 G_{vac}}{2 (6 G_{vac} + G_{vac})} = \frac{4}{14} = \frac{2}{7}.
\]

This is \emph{not} a fitted parameter. It is forced by two independent axiomatic inputs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Axiom~1 LC + Axiom~2 packing identity: the Feng--Thorpe--Garboczi EMT quadratic at packing fraction $p^* = 8\pi\alpha$ has the unique trace-reversal solution $K/G = 2$.
    \item Standard isotropic elastic identity: $\nu = (3K - 2G)/[2(3K + G)]$ evaluated at $K = 2G$ gives $\nu = 2/7$.
\end{itemize}

The derivation lives canonically at \texttt{ave-kb/vol3/gravity/ch01-gravity-yield/vacuum-poisson-ratio.md} (\texttt{clm-x19btt}) with the Effective Medium Theory derivation of $K/G = 2$ at the canonical leaves \texttt{topological-packing-fraction.md} and \texttt{one-seventh-impedance-projection.md} in the same KB directory. Per CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2, this is an Ax~1+Ax~2 \emph{interlink} (cross-axiom identity), not a fundamental axiom in its own right; the canonical-source entry is \texttt{src/ave/core/constants.py:447}.

The same $\nu_{vac} = 2/7$ propagates into downstream electroweak observables --- $\sin^2\theta_W = 1 - 7/9 = 2/9$ (on-shell, \texttt{src/ave/core/constants.py:478}); the Wolfenstein CKM parameterization with $\lambda = \sin^2\theta_W = 2/9$, $A = \cos\theta_W = \sqrt{7}/3$, $\sqrt{\rho^2 + \eta^2} = 1/\sqrt{7}$ (Vol~II Ch.~\ref{ch:electroweak}); and the PMNS mixing angles with $\sin^2\theta_{12} = \nu_{vac} + 1/45 \approx 0.308$ (\texttt{src/ave/core/constants.py:568}). \section{Cosserat Couple-Stress and the Weak-Force Range}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_couple_stress}

The Cosserat couple-stress modulus $\gamma_c$ is the substrate-native bending inductance of the microrotational sector: the bond-level resistance to differential rotation between neighbouring K4 nodes. The substrate-native characteristic length built from the couple-stress / shear-modulus ratio is

\[
l_c = \sqrt{\gamma_c / G_{vac}},
\]

and is identified with the weak-force range $r_W \approx 10^{-18}$~m (canonical at KB leaf \texttt{vol2/particle-physics/ch05-electroweak-mechanics/gauge-boson-masses.md} line~39, \texttt{clm-5zuo7g}; Vol~II Ch.~\ref{ch:electroweak}).

\textbf{Substrate mechanism.} Per the canonical derivation (Vol~II Ch.~\ref{ch:electroweak}; \texttt{gauge-boson-masses.md}): weak interactions lack the kinetic energy required to overcome the ambient LC rotational inductance. Any physical excitation operating \emph{below} the medium's natural cutoff frequency becomes an evanescent wave. The static field equation transforms from the Laplace equation $\nabla^2 \theta = 0$ to the massive Helmholtz equation $\nabla^2 \theta - \theta / l_c^2 = 0$, whose Green's function is the Yukawa potential $V_{weak}(r) \propto e^{-r/l_c}/r$. The $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ bosons are the evanescent cutoff modes of the substrate's bending and torsional sectors respectively, with mass ratio $m_W/m_Z = \sqrt{7}/3$ set by the same $\nu_{vac} = 2/7$ Poisson identity (\S\ref{sec:vol9_mech_poisson} above).

\textbf{EE-substrate-native projection} (\texttt{ave-ee-first-mapping} v1.0 means-test row~10): the Cosserat couple-stress modulus $\gamma_c$ \emph{is} the \textbf{transformer leakage inductance characteristic length} at the bond scale. The W/Z mass scale is set by the transformer-leakage-inductance characteristic frequency. The microrotational sector mass-gap $m_\omega \sim 1$~MeV (canonical at \texttt{common/trampoline-framework.md} line~188) is the \textbf{ferrite-Curie-temperature analog} for the substrate's magnetic-mode thermal-freeze threshold (means-test row~12).

The Vol~9 magnetic / microrotational characteristics of $\mathcal{M}_A$ --- the Cosserat flywheel inductance, the rotation-sector mass-gap, $B_{snap}$, and the weak-force range as a spec entry --- are documented separately in Ch.~10 (Magnetic / Microrotational Characteristics). This chapter records $\gamma_c$ as a bulk mechanical primitive; Ch.~10 develops the consequences for the magnetic sector.

\section{Mechanical Wave Speeds}
\label{sec:vol9_mech_wave_speeds}

The transverse and longitudinal mechanical wave speeds of $\mathcal{M}_A$ at the cold-lattice limit are set by the bulk moduli and density:

\[
v_T(0) = \sqrt{G_{vac} / \rho_{bulk}} = c_0,
\qquad
v_L(0) = \sqrt{K_{vac} / \rho_{bulk}} = \sqrt{2 G_{vac} / \rho_{bulk}} = \sqrt{2}\, c_0.
\]

The transverse speed equals $c_0$ \emph{by construction} --- photons are transverse excitations of the LC lattice, so $v_T = c_0$ identically (canonical-source \texttt{src/ave/core/constants.py:586--587}). The longitudinal speed $v_L = \sqrt{2}\, c_0$ follows from $K_{vac} = 2 G_{vac}$ (canonical-source \texttt{src/ave/core/constants.py:591}).

\textbf{Saturation modulation.} At finite saturation operating point $A_0 \neq 0$, the substrate carries two distinct effective speeds, and conflating them is the canonical Pitfall~\#5 framework-leakage error (CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2, caught 2026-05-28 Phase~3-A3 WALK-BACK):

\begin{itemize}
    \item $c_{EM}(A_0) = c_0 / S(A_0)$ --- the Maxwell phase velocity; appears in $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 \hbar c)$ and all Maxwell-equation derivations. Canonical at KB leaf \texttt{clm-8nkvwy}.
    \item $c_{shear}(A_0) = c_0\, \sqrt{S(A_0)}$ --- the substrate mechanical / group / rest-mass velocity; tracks the Schwarzschild $c\sqrt{1 - r_s/r}$ reduction in the SYM-class weak-field gravity limit. This is the spec entry for the mechanical $v_T$ at operating point $A_0$. Canonical at KB leaf \texttt{clm-8nkvwy}.
\end{itemize}

Both reduce to $c_0$ at the cold-lattice limit $S(0) = 1$. The mechanical-characteristics datasheet entry is $v_T(A_0) = c_{shear}(A_0) = c_0 \sqrt{S(A_0)}$. Under SYM-class saturation scaling (gravity-class realization: $\mu$ and $\varepsilon$ scale together so $Z_0$ stays invariant), this produces gravitational time dilation via the Schwarzschild reduction \emph{while} simultaneously preserving $\alpha$ exactly (canonical proof at \texttt{ave-kb/vol3/gravity/ch01-gravity-yield/alpha-invariance-symmetric-gravity.md}, \texttt{clm-3zz0f6}). The substrate-physics of the gravity-class SYM scaling is documented in Vol~3 Ch.~\ref{ch:gravity_and_yield}; this datasheet entry records $v_T(A_0) = c_{shear}(A_0)$ as the canonical mechanical wave-speed spec under saturation modulation.
