\section{The True Thermodynamic Phase Diagram}
\label{sec:vol9_true_phase_diagram}

\textbf{Why this section precedes the regime map.} A phase diagram, in the thermodynamic sense, partitions a plane of \emph{independent state variables} into regions of distinct material phase. The four-regime map of \S\ref{sec:vol9_phase_operating_point} is routinely \emph{called} the substrate's phase diagram, but its control parameter $r \equiv A/A_c$ is not an independent state variable --- it is an \emph{excitation} parameter (the local strain amplitude to which the solid crystal is driven along the Axiom 4 kernel). Driving $r$ upward is a \emph{trajectory} the solid is taken along; it is not a coordinate that separates one phase of matter from another. This section establishes the substrate's actual thermodynamic phase diagram --- the plane on which the solid, the melt, and the candidate cavitated phase are distinct regions, bounded by two failure lines --- after which \S\ref{sec:vol9_phase_operating_point} reads for what it is: the \emph{excitation map of the solid phase}.

\subsection{The axes argument: state variables vs.\ the excitation path}
\label{subsec:vol9_true_phase_axes}

The substrate is a saturable elastic K4--Cosserat crystal (Vol 1 Chs.~1--7). Like any crystal, its phase organization is set by independent thermodynamic state variables, not by how hard a given region is locally excited. Two state variables span the substrate's phase plane:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Thermal occupation $T$} (vertical axis) --- the bath temperature setting the mode population. At $T_{CMB} = \qty{2.725}{\kelvin}$ the substrate sits deep in its cold-lattice ground state (the standard cosmic phase, \S\ref{subsec:vol9_phase_standard}); raising $T$ toward $\sim m_e c^2/k_B$ thermally unfreezes the lightest topological excitation and the crystal loses coherent order.
    \item \textbf{Bulk dilatation $\bar\rho$} (horizontal axis) --- the mean compression/rarefaction of the substrate relative to its cold-lattice reference. Rarefying $\bar\rho$ toward the EOS softening root drives the bulk (compressional) wave speed $c_{bulk}^2 \to 0$, past which the medium can no longer support a compressional restoring force.
\end{itemize}

The control parameter $r = A/A_c$ is \emph{neither} of these. It is the operating-point amplitude of the Axiom 4 saturation kernel $S(r) = \sqrt{1 - r^2}$, which is a property the crystal exhibits \emph{at a point} when locally strained. The Regime I--IV progression is therefore an \emph{excursion along an excitation coordinate orthogonal to the $(T, \bar\rho)$ plane} --- the locus of how hard a point in the crystal is being driven, \emph{not} a displacement in $T$ or $\bar\rho$ --- and its terminus ($r \to 1$, $S \to 0$, local topology destroyed) carries that point \emph{into the melt phase}, the same phase reached globally by raising $T$ past the melt line. The $r$ coordinate is not an axis of this plane; it is orthogonal to both. Figure~\ref{fig:vol9_true_phase_diagram} draws the full plane: the solid region, the two failure lines, and --- in a \emph{separate inset axis} --- the Regime I--IV excitation coordinate $r$, which is orthogonal to both plotted state variables (driving $r$ at a point raises neither $T$ nor $\bar\rho$); its terminus $r \to 1$ (local rupture) lands \emph{in the melt phase}, reached at any bath $T$.

\subsection{Proof: the regime axis is defined only inside the crystal}
\label{subsec:vol9_true_phase_proof}

The saturation kernel $S(r) = \sqrt{1 - r^2}$ is the Axiom 4 response of the K4--Cosserat \emph{solid}, parametrized by the solid's own local strain amplitude $r = A/A_c$. There is no K4 bond, no critical threshold $A_c$, and therefore no $r$ and no $S$, in the absence of the lattice. The regime axis presupposes the crystal.

But the substrate has a phase that \emph{predates} the crystal. At lattice genesis the K4 crystal crystallized \emph{out of} a hotter, disordered parent medium --- the pre-geodesic plasma. Per canonical \kbleaf{manuscript/ave-kb/common/cosmic-axes-and-frames-glossary.md}:73 (quoting \kbleaf{04\_generative\_cosmology.tex}:405--413):

\begin{quote}
\itshape ``\ldots the inside region is our universe's pre-crystallization phase (supercooled pre-geodesic plasma).''
\end{quote}

A coordinate that is defined only \emph{after} the crystal exists cannot be the axis on which the \emph{pre-crystal} phase is located. The melt (pre-geodesic plasma) has no $r$, no $A_c$, no Regime number --- it is the phase the regime axis itself is built on top of. Therefore the four-regime $r$-map \textbf{cannot} be the substrate's thermodynamic phase diagram; it is the excitation map of a single phase, the solid. The true phase diagram must be drawn on axes ($T$, $\bar\rho$) on which the solid, the melt, and the cavitated phase are all simultaneously representable as regions.
