\section{Framing: the Datasheet as a Simulator Spec}
\label{sec:vol9_engine_framing}

The preceding chapters characterize the vacuum cell as a component: its DOFs (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_pin_port_configuration}), its couplings (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_pin_port_configuration} \S Sector-to-Sector Coupling Matrix), its absolute limits (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_absolute_maximum_ratings}), its small-signal and temporal characteristics (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_ac_electrical_characteristics}). This chapter reads the same datasheet as a specification for the \emph{simulator}: what a numerical engine must implement, per datasheet line, to render one cell faithfully.

The discipline here is empirical, not aspirational. Every requirement below is mapped to (a) the datasheet line it enforces and (b) a \emph{documented engine failure} that taught it --- a run where an engine that violated the requirement produced a wrong or null result. The substrate is natural; the engine is engineered; and the engine-failure history is the empirical record of which substrate features a simulator cannot omit. Per the substrate-native-check discipline, SM/QED defaults (Lagrangian gradient-descent, continuum-Helmholtz, energy-basin minimization, bulk-force confinement) leak in unless these requirements are stated and enforced.

\section{Conservation Canaries}
\label{sec:vol9_engine_conservation_canaries}

Requirements~4 and~6 share a detector: the conservation canaries that distinguish a conservative \emph{energize-and-lock} coupling from a non-conservative \emph{pump}. A faithful cross-sector coupling sources one sector from another and takes the back-reaction from the same $H_{couple}$ --- energy moves between sectors but the total is conserved. A pump (the flag-gated EMF, requirement~4) adds energy that no back-reaction removes.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hamiltonian drift.} On a conservative run, the total $H$ is constant to integrator order; the canary is $|\Delta H / H| \sim 10^{-9}$ per step on float64 (Velocity-Verlet / leapfrog). A pump shows monotonic $H$ growth orders of magnitude above the float64 canary floor. \emph{This is why requirement~6 mandates float64}: on float32 the round-off drift ($\sim 10^{-7}$) swamps the $10^{-9}$ signal and the pump-vs-lock distinction is lost.
    \item \textbf{Total angular-momentum bound.} The microrotational sector's $|\mathbf{L}|$ must stay bounded with $d|\mathbf{L}|^2/dt \approx 0$ on a locked run. The EMF pump (\kbleaf{src/ave/topological/k4\_cosserat\_coupling.py}:550, positive-sign) drives $|\mathbf{L}|$ unbounded --- the genesis-24 detonation signature. The $|\mathbf{L}|$ gate is the direct read of requirement~4.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\textbf{The energize-and-lock principle.} A stable bound state is built by energizing a conserved invariant and \emph{locking} it (boundary-confined, $\Gamma = -1$), not by pumping it. The deepest lesson of the genesis arc is that an amplifying cross-sector term feels like progress (it makes amplitude grow) but is the failure mode: it never reaches a stable lock because it never conserves. An engine that passes both canaries on a closed run --- $H$-drift at the float64 floor, $|\mathbf{L}|^2$ flat --- is energizing-and-locking; one that fails either is pumping. The datasheet records the canaries so that the distinction is checkable, not assumed.
