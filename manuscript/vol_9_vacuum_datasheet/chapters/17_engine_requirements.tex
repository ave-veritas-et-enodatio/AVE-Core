\section{Framing: the Datasheet as a Simulator Spec}
\label{sec:vol9_engine_framing}

The preceding chapters characterize the vacuum cell as a component: its DOFs (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_pin_port_configuration}), its couplings (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_pin_port_configuration} \S Sector-to-Sector Coupling Matrix), its absolute limits (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_absolute_maximum_ratings}), its small-signal and temporal characteristics (Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_ac_electrical_characteristics}). This chapter reads the same datasheet as a specification for the \emph{simulator}: what a numerical engine must implement, per datasheet line, to render one cell faithfully.

Each requirement is stated as the substrate feature a simulator cannot omit, together with the failure mode an engine exhibits if it omits it. The substrate is natural; the engine is engineered. Per the substrate-native-check discipline, SM/QED defaults (Lagrangian gradient-descent, continuum-Helmholtz, energy-basin minimization, bulk-force confinement) leak in unless these requirements are stated and enforced.

\section{Conservation Canaries}
\label{sec:vol9_engine_conservation_canaries}

Requirements~4 and~6 share a detector: the conservation canaries that distinguish a conservative \emph{energize-and-lock} coupling from a non-conservative \emph{pump}. A faithful cross-sector coupling sources one sector from another and takes the back-reaction from the same $H_{couple}$ --- energy moves between sectors but the total is conserved. A pump (the amplifying EMF coupling, requirement~4) adds energy that no back-reaction removes.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hamiltonian drift.} On a conservative run, the total $H$ is constant to integrator order; the canary is $|\Delta H / H| \sim 10^{-9}$ per step on float64 (Velocity-Verlet / leapfrog). A pump shows monotonic $H$ growth orders of magnitude above the float64 canary floor. \emph{This is why requirement~6 mandates float64}: on float32 the round-off drift ($\sim 10^{-7}$) swamps the $10^{-9}$ signal and the pump-vs-lock distinction is lost.
    \item \textbf{Total angular-momentum bound.} The microrotational sector's $|\mathbf{L}|$ must stay bounded with $d|\mathbf{L}|^2/dt \approx 0$ on a locked run. A \emph{wrong-sign} ($+2$) EMF coupling drives the $V$-sector energy and $|\mathbf{L}|$ into a monotone runaway --- the detonation signature. The Lenz-correct $-2$ (\kbleaf{k4\_cosserat\_coupling.py}:838) removes the $V$-sector detonation (bounded $E_V$, \texttt{reverses}\,$=$\,\texttt{True}), but on the same deep-saturated seed $|\mathbf{L}|$ still transiently spikes before relaxing (no longer pinned, yet above the $5\times$ bound) so the full ledger does not \emph{fully} close --- the $-2$ is a $V$-sector-energy correction, not a demonstrated lock; it remains gated off for the Op14 varactor double-count (sign-independent). The $|\mathbf{L}|$ gate is the direct read of requirement~4.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\textbf{The energize-and-lock principle.} A stable bound state is built by energizing a conserved invariant and \emph{locking} it (boundary-confined, $\Gamma = -1$), not by pumping it. An amplifying cross-sector term grows the amplitude but never reaches a stable lock because it never conserves --- it is a failure mode, not a binding mechanism. An engine that passes both canaries on a closed run --- $H$-drift at the float64 floor, $|\mathbf{L}|^2$ flat --- is energizing-and-locking; one that fails either is pumping. The datasheet records the canaries so that the distinction is checkable, not assumed.
