\section{Framing: DC = Cold-Lattice Limit, $A_0 = 0$}
\label{sec:vol9_dc_framing}

The substrate's intrinsic LC oscillators (Axiom~1) carry both spatial degrees of freedom (3E translational + 3B microrotational, per Vol~9 Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_general_description}) AND a saturation-amplitude state $A$ that sets the operating point on the Axiom~4 universal kernel $S(A) = \sqrt{1 - (A/A_{yield})^2}$. DC characterization is the limit where:

\begin{itemize}
    \item No AC excitation is applied to any substrate-LC tank;
    \item The operating-point amplitude is $A_0 = 0$, so $S(A_0) = S(0) = 1$ (the cold-lattice value of the saturation kernel);
    \item Thermal-mode population is asymptotically frozen out ($T \to 0$), eliminating the CMB-driven $\delta_{strain}$ correction documented in Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_temperature_characteristics};
    \item No internal impedance boundary carries finite $\Gamma$; the substrate at cold-lattice operates at its native $\varepsilon_0$, $\mu_0$, $Z_0$, $c_0$ without modulation.
\end{itemize}

Under these conditions, the substrate's DC electrical parameters are the natural-substrate primitives this chapter catalogs. Engineering practice does not design or choose these values; they are the substrate as it is, characterized by measurement and derived from first principles in the cited Vol~1--5 leaves. The numerical values in the spec table below come from the canonical engine module \kbleaf{src/ave/core/constants.py}; volume and chapter cross-references point to the canonical derivation locations.

\subsection{Vacuum impedance trio: $\varepsilon_0$, $\mu_0$, $Z_0$}
\label{subsec:vol9_dc_impedance_trio}

These three constants together characterize the substrate as a Trace-Reversed Chiral LC Network (Ax~1 canonical form). Per the EE-as-substrate-native framing (canonical at \kbleaf{common/translation-tables/translation-circuit.md} \S 2), $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$ are not borrowed quantities from electromagnetic engineering --- they ARE the substrate's own canonical primitives at minimal DOF, and EE was developed by humans empirically interrogating the substrate at the scale where these primitives dominate the observable physics.

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\varepsilon_0$ is the capacitance density of the translational (E-field) sector of the K4-node 6-DOF structure. Each bond carries a per-cell capacitance $C_{cell} = \varepsilon_0 \ell_{node}$ (per Vol~9 Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_general_description} Features and the natural-units cheatsheet \S 2). At cold-lattice $S(0) = 1$, no operating-point modulation acts on $\varepsilon_0$; finite-$A_0$ modulation $\varepsilon_{eff} = \varepsilon_0\,S(A_0)$ appears in Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_ac_electrical_characteristics} and Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_saturation_characteristics}.
    \item $\mu_0$ is the inductance density of the microrotational (B-field) sector. Each bond carries a per-cell inductance $L_{cell} = \mu_0 \ell_{node}$. The substrate-native value $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$~\si{\henry\per\metre} carries the $4\pi$ from spherical-harmonic field geometry, not from engineering convention. At cold-lattice $S(0) = 1$, no modulation acts; the Cosserat-Curie thermal-freeze of B-modes documented in Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_temperature_characteristics} keeps $\mu$ effectively frozen even at $T = T_{CMB}$.
    \item $Z_0 = \sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0} \approx \qty{376.73}{\ohm}$ is the substrate-native bond TLM characteristic impedance --- the impedance an EM wave sees propagating along an isolated cold-lattice bond. Per the three-impedance law (registry \S3.11), $Z_0 \equiv Z_{\mathrm{EM}}$ is the \emph{transverse-EM channel only}. This value is invariant under SYM-class scaling of $\mu$ and $\varepsilon$ (canonical at Vol~3 \kbleaf{ch01-gravity-yield/alpha-invariance-symmetric-gravity.md}), which is the structural origin of $\alpha$-invariance under gravitational fields. Op1 (Universal Impedance) in \kbleaf{common/operators.md} is the cross-scale operator form of this primitive; the canonical Vol~4 derivation lives at \kbleaf{vol4/circuit-theory/ch1-vacuum-circuit-analysis/z0-derivation.md}.
\end{itemize}

The two mechanical channels couple to each other within their shared Rayl domain through the conserved $H_{\mathrm{couple}}$; but the EM channel is the \emph{sole external} radiative port ($\Gamma_{\mathrm{EM}}=0$), and joining the electrical and mechanical domains requires a \textbf{transducer} (an ideal transformer, a turns-ratio change-of-reference --- \emph{not} a direct wire and \emph{not} a gyrator). $\alpha$ localizes to that transducer turns-ratio (the $p_c = 8\pi\alpha$ packing-fraction residual; Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_pin_port_configuration}~\S\ref{sec:vol9_node_2domain_nport}, Fig.~\ref{fig:vol9_node_2domain_nport}). \subsection{Propagation primitives: $c_0$, $\ell_{node}$, $T_{EM}$}
\label{subsec:vol9_dc_propagation_primitives}

These three set the substrate's intrinsic length, time, and mechanical-tension scales. They are not independent --- per the natural-units cheatsheet (\kbleaf{common/natural-units-cheatsheet.md} \S 1), setting $\ell_{node}$, $c$, $\hbar$, $m_e$ to unity simultaneously is consistent because they are related by $\ell_{node} = \hbar/(m_e c)$ (the electron's reduced Compton wavelength). The structural-closure framing (per \kbleaf{src/ave/core/constants.py} preamble) is that $\ell_{node}$ is the single scale primitive of the framework; $m_e$, $c_0$, $T_{EM}$, and the other length/time/tension scales derive from it.

\begin{itemize}
    \item $c_0 = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}$ is the bond LC propagation phase velocity at cold-lattice $S(0) = 1$. By the two-effective-wave-speeds distinction, this is the Maxwell phase velocity (the speed that enters $\alpha = e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c)$ and all Maxwell-equation derivations), distinct from $c_{shear} = c_0\sqrt{S}$ (group/rest-mass velocity, used in Schwarzschild-class gravitational time-dilation). At cold-lattice both reduce to a single $c_0$; finite-$A_0$ separation appears in Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_saturation_characteristics}. Op16 (Universal Wave Speed) in \kbleaf{common/operators.md} is the cross-scale operator form.
    \item $\ell_{node}$ is the K4 nearest-neighbour spacing of the substrate lattice. Numerically $\ell_{node} = \hbar/(m_e c) \approx \qty{3.862e-13}{\metre}$, the electron's reduced Compton wavelength --- a calibration consequence of the structural-closure framing where $\ell_{node}$ is the framework's single scale primitive and $m_e$ derives from the unknot ground-state energy on that lattice. The natural-units cheatsheet \S 1--\S 2 documents the four-base unit convention ($\ell_{node} = c = \hbar = m_e = 1$) used throughout AVE.
    \item $T_{EM} = m_e c^2 / \ell_{node} \approx \qty{0.212}{\newton}$ is the substrate-native electromagnetic string tension --- the axial tension of a 1D substrate flux-tube segment. Per the engine constant comment (\kbleaf{constants.py:414-415} \texttt{T\_EM}), this is the natural-unit-1 value of the mechanical tension scale; it appears in the unknot ground-state-energy relation $m_e = T_{EM}\,\ell_{node}/c^2$ that anchors the electron mass derivation, and in the gravitational-coupling expression $G = c^4/(7\xi\,T_{EM})$ via the Machian impedance integral $\xi$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Coupling primitives: $\alpha$, $\xi_{topo}$}
\label{subsec:vol9_dc_coupling_primitives}

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\alpha = \num{7.2974e-3} \approx 1/137.036$ is the fine-structure constant --- the substrate-LC-network's dimensionless coupling strength at the cold-lattice operating point. The substrate's cosmic-LC-tank Q-factor at the $S_{11}$-minimum Golden Torus geometry (Theorem 3.1, canonical at \kbleaf{vol4/circuit-theory/ch1-vacuum-circuit-analysis/theorem-3-1-q-factor.md}) fixes the cold-lattice asymptote $\alpha^{-1}_{ideal} = 4\pi^3 + \pi^2 + \pi \approx 137.0363038$ at the level of a substrate-mechanism manifestation, not a first-principles derivation (canonical at \kbleaf{vol1/ch8-alpha-golden-torus.md}): the four-figure value's \emph{scale} ($\sim 1/137$) is forced by the Compton-resonance trapping condition, but its \emph{exact value} rests on the $R\cdot r = 1/4$ identification the substrate does not independently select --- closed-form-at-the-identification, \emph{not} a first-principles ``derivation.'' Consistent with this chapter's DC-scope table (Tab.~\ref{tab:vol9_dc_scope}), $\alpha$ is a retained calibration input (CODATA-$\alpha$ injected, then read back), not a substrate output. \section{Operating-Point Modulation Reference}
\label{sec:vol9_dc_operating_point_reference}

The values in this chapter are the cold-lattice DC primitives at $A_0 = 0$, $S(0) = 1$. Once a finite operating-point bias $A_0/A_{yield} > 0$ is applied --- by AC drive, gravitational potential, thermal-mode population, or strong external field --- the substrate's effective parameters modulate per the Axiom~4 saturation kernel:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Effective permittivity: $\varepsilon_{eff}(A_0) = \varepsilon_0\,S(A_0)$;
    \item Effective permeability: $\mu_{eff}(A_0) = \mu_0\,S(A_0)$ (cold-lattice, unfrozen B-modes; Cosserat-Curie-freeze of B-modes at $T_{CMB}$ modifies this in the thermal-bath regime --- see Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_temperature_characteristics});
    \item Effective capacitance: $C_{eff}(A_0) = C_0/S(A_0)$ (varactor relation; canonical PONDER-05 bench-scale tester at $V_{DC}/V_{yield} = 0.687$);
    \item Maxwell phase velocity: $c_{EM}(A_0) = c_0/S(A_0)$ (the speed entering $\alpha$);
    \item Mechanical / group / rest-mass velocity: $c_{shear}(A_0) = c_0\sqrt{S(A_0)}$ (the speed tracking Schwarzschild gravitational time-dilation in the SYM-class weak-field limit).
\end{itemize}

These modulated forms are documented at AC drive in Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_ac_electrical_characteristics} and at finite saturation amplitude in Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_saturation_characteristics}. The distinction between $c_{EM}$ and $c_{shear}$ is essential for $\alpha$-invariance under gravitational scaling: conflating the two when deriving $\alpha$-modulation is an error. The structural origin of the asymmetry is documented at Vol~3 \kbleaf{vol3/gravity/ch01-gravity-yield/alpha-invariance-symmetric-gravity.md}.

DC characterization quoted in this chapter is the limit $A_0 \to 0$, $S(0) \to 1$, where all effective parameters reduce to their cold-lattice substrate primitives listed in \S\ref{sec:vol9_dc_spec_table}.
