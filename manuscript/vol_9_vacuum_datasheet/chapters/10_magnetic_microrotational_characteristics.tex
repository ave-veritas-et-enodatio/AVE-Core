\section{Microrotational Sector Identity}
\label{sec:vol9_microrotational_sector_identity}

The magnetic sector of the substrate is the rotational half of those 6 DOFs / node:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Three \textbf{microrotational} DOFs per K4 node --- each node carries an independent Cosserat micro-rotation coordinate $\boldsymbol{\omega}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ in addition to the translational displacement $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{r}, t)$. The three components $\omega_x$, $\omega_y$, $\omega_z$ are not derived from $\mathbf{u}$ (as would be the case in classical Cauchy elasticity, where $\boldsymbol{\omega} = \tfrac{1}{2}\nabla \times \mathbf{u}$) --- they are \emph{independent} kinematic degrees of freedom, the defining feature of a Cosserat micropolar continuum.
    \item \textbf{Inductive coupling} $\mu_0$ --- the substrate's bond-level inductance density. Each bond's inductance $L_{cell} = \mu_0 \ell_{node}$ stores energy in the microrotational sector exactly as the bond capacitance $C_{cell} = \varepsilon_0 \ell_{node}$ stores energy in the translational sector.
    \item \textbf{Magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$} --- the substrate-observable signature of microrotational density. Macroscopic $\mathbf{B}$ is the coarse-grained microrotation field; the magnetic field is not abstracted from a more primitive mechanical quantity, it IS the substrate's Cosserat rotational state, observed at engineering scale.
    \item \textbf{Substrate-native origin of intrinsic spin} --- the same Cosserat microrotational DOF, integrated across an extended closed soliton defect, produces the SU(2) / SO(3) double-cover spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ representation classically via the Finkelstein--Misner kink mechanism (canonical at \texttt{vol2/particle-physics/ch01-topological-matter/finkelstein-misner-spin-half-derivation.md}, \texttt{clm-salw2h}). Macroscopic angular momentum, the electromagnetic $\mathbf{B}$ field, and quantum spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ are three projections of the same per-node rotational coordinate. See Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_topological_characteristics} \S Topological Spec Table for the spin-closure spec ($4\pi$ for extended closed defects; $2\pi$ for open propagating modes) and the canonical $T \to 2T \subset SU(2)$ group-theoretic chain.
\end{itemize}

This is the load-bearing substrate-physics distinction between $\mathcal{M}_A$ and a classical-elastic (Cauchy) continuum: a Cauchy continuum has translational DOFs only, no independent micro-rotation, no native magnetic-field analog, and no native spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ representation. The substrate is Cosserat micropolar by Axiom~1; that one structural feature furnishes the entire B-sector. The substrate is natural; the microrotational DOFs are intrinsic to the substrate's K4-Cosserat structure; engineering observes them collectively as $\mathbf{B}$.

\section{Cosserat Flywheel Inductance}
\label{sec:vol9_cosserat_flywheel_inductance}

The bond-level inductance of $\mathcal{M}_A$ is the microrotational analog of bond capacitance. Each K4 bond connecting nearest-neighbour nodes carries

\[
L_{cell} = \mu_0\, \ell_{node}, \qquad C_{cell} = \varepsilon_0\, \ell_{node},
\]

with $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$~\si{\henry\per\metre} the substrate-native bond microrotation density (canonical-source \texttt{src/ave/core/constants.py:79}, \texttt{MU\_0}). Per Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_dc_electrical_characteristics} \S Vacuum impedance trio, the $4\pi$ prefactor on $\mu_0$ carries the spherical-harmonic field-geometry origin, not engineering convention; the substrate-native value is identical to the SI defined value because SI inherits the substrate.

\textbf{Flywheel picture.} Each K4 node carries a Cosserat micro-rotation $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ with a Cosserat micro-inertia $I_\omega$; the bond's inductance is the substrate-native resistance to changing the per-bond microrotation differential, exactly as a transformer's mutual inductance is the resistance to changing the flux linkage between its windings. Engineering practice catalogs the flywheel storage at the LC tank level (oscillator $\omega_C = 1/\sqrt{L_{cell} C_{cell}} = c_0/\ell_{node}$, per Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_ac_electrical_characteristics} \S AC characteristic frequency); AVE substrate-physics derives the inductance as the microrotation-density side of the LC tank.

\textbf{Cosserat micro-inertia $I_\omega$ at bond scale.} The Cosserat continuum carries two independent kinematic PDEs (canonical at \texttt{common/trampoline-framework.md} line~181):

\[
\rho\, \ddot{\mathbf{u}} = \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} + \mathbf{f},
\qquad
I_\omega\, \ddot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} = \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{couple}} + 2\sigma^A + \mathbf{g},
\]

where $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ is the (asymmetric) Cauchy stress tensor with antisymmetric part $\sigma^A$, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{couple}}$ is the couple-stress tensor (units N/m), and $I_\omega$ is the Cosserat micro-inertia. The asymmetric stress $\sigma^A$ is the substrate-native coupling between translational and microrotational sectors: bond stretching $u$ produces antisymmetric stress, which in turn drives microrotation $\omega$, which in turn induces neighbouring micro-rotations through the couple-stress $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{couple}}$. The translational $\rho$-equation and the rotational $I_\omega$-equation are bidirectionally coupled.

The substrate $\mathbf{B}$-field is the spatial coarse-graining of $\boldsymbol{\omega}$; Faraday's law of induction is the substrate-native consequence of bidirectional translational-rotational coupling: time-varying $\mathbf{E}$ (translation of charge) drives time-varying $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ (microrotation), with the cross-coupling captured by the $2\sigma^A$ term in the rotational PDE. The EE Faraday law $\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\partial_t \mathbf{B}$ is the substrate's Cosserat-bidirectional-coupling identity projected onto the macroscopic LC continuum.

\section{Rotation-Sector Mass-Gap and Ferrite-Curie Analog}
\label{sec:vol9_rotation_sector_mass_gap}

The Cosserat microrotational sector carries a finite excitation gap. Per the canonical Verlet-validated derivation (\texttt{common/trampoline-framework.md} line~188; E-046 canonical):

\[
m_\omega^2 = 4\, G_c / I_\omega,
\]

with $G_c$ the Cosserat couple-stress modulus (here called $\gamma_c$ in the spec table; the trampoline-framework leaf uses $G_c$) and $I_\omega$ the Cosserat micro-inertia. The natural-units period is $T = 2\pi / \omega_m = \pi$. The empirical scale at substrate parameters is $m_\omega \sim \qty{1}{\mega\electronvolt}$ --- approximately the rest-energy scale of the lightest charged lepton (electron) and the canonical reference value used in the means-test corpus (row~12 of \texttt{ave-ee-first-mapping} v1.0).

\textbf{Ferrite-Curie thermal analog.} The EE-substrate-native projection of the rotation-sector mass-gap is the \textbf{ferrite Curie temperature}: below $T_C^{\text{ferrite}}$, the magnetic moments of a ferrite ceramic align into domains and the magnetic-response (permeability $\mu$) is finite; above $T_C^{\text{ferrite}}$, thermal energy disrupts the domain structure and $\mu$ collapses. The substrate analog: below $T \sim m_\omega/k_B \sim 10^{10}$~K, microrotational modes are thermally frozen out --- but \emph{the freeze runs the opposite direction relative to ferrite Curie behavior} in the regime relevant to all current astrophysics. At engineering operating temperatures including $T_{CMB} = 2.725$~K, the substrate sits far below the rotation-sector mass-gap and B-modes are frozen: $\mu$ is locked at $\mu_0$, no thermal modulation of the microrotational sector. The substrate operates naturally at the \emph{deep below-mass-gap} side of the curve at every laboratory temperature; this is not an engineering design choice but a structural consequence of the substrate's natural Cosserat couple-stress modulus $\gamma_c$ and Cosserat micro-inertia $I_\omega$ setting $m_\omega$ above the energy scale of any reachable thermal bath.

This asymmetric thermal response --- $\varepsilon$ thermally modulated (E-modes thermally populated; no rest-mass-equivalent gap for translational modes at substrate scale) while $\mu$ thermally frozen (B-modes mass-gap-suppressed) --- produces the canonical $\delta_{strain}$ mechanism at $T_{CMB}$ documented in Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_temperature_characteristics}. The Cosserat-Curie-frozen $\mu$ side is the substrate-native origin of the thermal-mode-population ASYM class identified as a new substrate-mechanism class beyond SYM / strong-field ASYM (per CLAUDE.md INVARIANT-S2 footnote on Q-DELTA-MAP-1 closure at mechanism-class identification, 2026-05-28).

\section{Weak-Force Range and W/Z Cutoff}
\label{sec:vol9_weak_force_range_wz_cutoff}

The Cosserat couple-stress modulus $\gamma_c$ and the bulk shear modulus $G_{vac}$ together set the substrate-native characteristic length

\[
l_c = \sqrt{\gamma_c / G_{vac}},
\]

which is identified with the weak-force range $r_W \approx 10^{-18}$~m (canonical at \texttt{vol2/particle-physics/ch05-electroweak-mechanics/gauge-boson-masses.md} line~39, \texttt{clm-5zuo7g}; see Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_mechanical_characteristics} \S Cosserat Couple-Stress for the matching mechanical-side spec).

\textbf{Substrate mechanism} (per the canonical Vol~II derivation, \texttt{clm-5zuo7g} + \texttt{clm-q8un7j}). Weak interactions lack the kinetic energy required to overcome the ambient LC rotational inductance. Any physical excitation operating below the medium's natural cutoff frequency becomes an \textbf{evanescent wave}. The static field equation for the microrotational phase $\theta$ transforms from the Laplace equation $\nabla^2 \theta = 0$ to the massive Helmholtz equation $\nabla^2 \theta - \theta / l_c^2 = 0$, whose Green's function is the Yukawa potential $V_{weak}(r) \propto e^{-r/l_c}/r$.

\textbf{$W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ identification.} The gauge bosons of the weak interaction are the substrate's evanescent cutoff modes in two distinct geometric channels:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $W^\pm$ --- pure longitudinal-torsional evanescent mode of the rotation sector; characteristic wavenumber $k \propto G_{vac} J$ (torsional stiffness $\times$ polar moment of inertia of the bond cross-section).
    \item $Z^0$ --- transverse-bending evanescent mode of the rotation sector; characteristic wavenumber $k \propto E_{vac} I$ (bending stiffness $\times$ second moment of area).
\end{itemize}

For a substrate-bond cylinder of diameter $d = \ell_{node}$ (Axiom~1 hard-sphere exclusion limit), the Perpendicular Axis Theorem fixes $J = 2 I$ identically. The mass ratio reduces to

\[
m_W / m_Z = \sqrt{2 G_{vac} / E_{vac}},
\]

and the standard isotropic-elastic identity $E = 2 G (1 + \nu)$ at $\nu = \nu_{vac} = 2/7$ produces

\[
m_W / m_Z = \sqrt{2 G / [2 G (1 + 2/7)]} = \sqrt{7/9} = \sqrt{7}/3 \approx 0.882.
\]

The on-shell Weinberg angle is $\sin^2 \theta_W = 1 - m_W^2/m_Z^2 = 1 - 7/9 = 2/9$ (canonical-source \texttt{src/ave/core/constants.py:478}; see Ch.~\ref{ch:vol9_mechanical_characteristics} \S Vacuum Poisson Ratio for the downstream electroweak observables propagating from $\nu_{vac} = 2/7$).

\textbf{EE-substrate-native projection} (means-test row~10 of \texttt{ave-ee-first-mapping} v1.0): the Cosserat couple-stress modulus $\gamma_c$ \emph{is} the \textbf{transformer leakage-inductance characteristic length} at the bond scale. Weak interactions are the substrate-physics manifestation of below-cutoff coupling between two LC tanks separated by a Cosserat-rotational coupling; the same below-cutoff evanescent-wave mathematics governs both.
