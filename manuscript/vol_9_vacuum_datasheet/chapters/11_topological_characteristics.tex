\section{Torus-Knot Soliton Winding}
\label{sec:vol9_torus_knot_winding}

Stable matter sectors are topological solitons on $\mathcal{M}_A$. The canonical taxonomy is the $(p, q)$ torus-knot family, classified by two integer windings:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $p$ --- toroidal-direction winding number
    \item $q$ --- poloidal-direction winding number
    \item $\gcd(p, q) = 1$ (coprimality required for a single-component closed knot, not a multi-component link)
    \item $p, q \geq 2$ (both windings non-trivial for a true knot rather than an unknot)
\end{itemize}

Per \texttt{phase-space-coordinate-check} v1.0 discipline, this is NOT to be remapped into SM gauge-group coordinates: the $(2, 3)$ winding is a substrate topological invariant on the Clifford torus, not a gauge-group representation. The Clifford-torus coordinates and the K4-graph real-space coordinates are the load-bearing substrate-coordinate systems for the electron; both are preserved as substrate-native primary.

\section{SU(2) / SO(3) Double Cover and Cosserat Spin-Half}
\label{sec:vol9_su2_so3_double_cover}

Intrinsic spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ is the substrate-native consequence of the Cosserat microrotational DOF integrated across the extended $0_1$ unknot defect. The standard $SU(2) / SO(3)$ double-cover --- single-valuedness on $SU(2)$, double-valuedness on $SO(3)$ --- arises classically on the K4 substrate via the Finkelstein--Misner kink mechanism (1959 differential topology; equivalent to the Dirac belt-trick) without invoking quantum-mechanical postulates.

The mechanism: an extended closed defect (the $0_1$ unknot at minimum ropelength $2\pi$, embedded across multiple K4 bonds) acquires a topologically non-trivial twist under a $2\pi$ rotation of its environment relative to the lattice. The twist cannot be removed without cutting the loop. Under a $4\pi$ rotation, the accumulated twist unwinds through the loop's body and the embedding returns to its initial state. Extended objects connected to their environment have a 2-to-1 cover of the $SO(3)$ rotation group --- this is classical topology of extended objects in 3D, demonstrable with any belt or rope.

The group-theoretic anchor: the K4 lattice's rotation group is the tetrahedral group $T = A_4$ (order 12). The binary tetrahedral group $2T$ (order 24) sits inside $SU(2)$ as a discrete subgroup, with exact sequence $1 \to \mathbb{Z}_2 \to 2T \to A_4 \to 1$. The Cosserat microrotational DOF on the K4 lattice transforms under $2T$ rather than $T$ when integrated across the extended unknot defect; this is what realizes the substrate-native $SU(2)$ structure. A $2\pi$ rotation in $SO(3)$ lifts to $-I \in SU(2)$ (sign flip); only $4\pi$ lifts to $+I$.

The mechanism is bench-verifiable in continuum mechanics: any belt-trick demonstration is a direct macroscopic analogue. The substrate analogue is the same topology applied to the $0_1$ unknot extended defect on the K4 carrier. Open propagating modes (photons, transverse EM waves) do NOT inherit the $4\pi$ closure --- they are not closed extended defects, do not satisfy the FM-kink hypothesis, and live at the $S^2$ projection level of the Hopf fibration where the $U(1)$ fibre phase carrying $4\pi$ closure has been integrated out.
