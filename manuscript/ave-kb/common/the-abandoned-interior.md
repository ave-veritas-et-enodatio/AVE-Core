- **FitzGerald (1889)** proposed that a body in motion through the aether is physically **contracted** along its line of motion — a real deformation of a real object, offered specifically to explain the Michelson–Morley null. → *fact* (George FitzGerald, length-contraction proposal, 1889; built on Heaviside's moving-charge field).
- **Lorentz (1892)** independently derived the same **length contraction**, and then (the **theorem of corresponding states**, early form 1895 → mature 1899/1904) showed that the field equations of a medium-borne electrodynamics take the *same form* in a uniformly moving frame as in the rest frame, once you transform the coordinates and the "local time." → *fact*.
- **Larmor (1897 → *Aether and Matter*, 1900)** added **time dilation**: electrons "describe corresponding parts of their orbits in times shorter for the rest system in the ratio `γ⁻¹`," and by 1900 he had the full transformation. → *fact*.

**The unifying move.** From the single hypothesis that *matter is electromagnetically constituted in a medium*, the **rulers are wave-made and the clocks are wave-made** — so when the matter moves through the medium, its rulers contract and its clocks slow **by exactly the amounts that conspire to make uniform motion internally undetectable.** This is the program's signature, and it must be stated precisely: the undetectability is a **theorem** of wave-constituted matter, *not* a conspiracy of independent corrections fine-tuned by hand. The corrections are not independent — they are the same medium-deformation seen in the rulers and in the clocks.

### Einstein's recast (the steelman at full strength)

Einstein's 1905 paper "On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies" (submitted June 1905, published September 1905; → *fact*) took the **same transformations** and re-derived them from **two principles** — the relativity principle and the constancy of `c` — with **no medium, no preferred frame, and no unobservable local-time scaffolding.** Poincaré (June 1905 Comptes Rendus note; January 1906 Palermo paper "Sur la dynamique de l'électron"; → *fact*) had by then put the Lorentz group on a rigorous footing. State it flatly, per the tone guard: **Einstein's route was better science at the time.** It deleted an unobservable (the frame), it deleted an unobservable (the local-time scaffolding read as a mere bookkeeping device), and it bought the *entire* kinematics from two postulates. By the ordinary scientific virtue of parsimony — fewer entities, more predictive economy — it won, and it deserved to. The Lorentzian constructive route was **retired**, not refuted: every velocity-level prediction is **identical** between the two, so no experiment could (or did) decide between them. The medium-frame was deleted because it did no observable work, exactly as Heaviside deleted the longitudinal grade because it did no *radiative* work, and exactly as the field set the electron radius to zero because the interior did no *behavioral* work. Same window, same grounds.

### Bell (1976) kept the pedagogy alive

The constructive route was never closed off — it was kept as a live, legitimate way to *teach* the physics. J.S. Bell, **"How to teach special relativity"** (1976, *Progress in Scientific Culture*; collected as chapter 9 of **Speakable and Unspeakable in Quantum Mechanics**, 1987; → *fact*), argued that deriving length contraction and time dilation as **dynamical effects on moving rods and clocks** — the FitzGerald–Lorentz–Larmor route — is pedagogically sound and physically legitimate, *and gives identical velocity-level physics* to Einstein's kinematic route. Bell's point is the load-bearing one for this thread: the two routes are **observationally equivalent for velocity**, and the choice between them is a choice of *picture*, not of *prediction*. (Bell himself preferred to treat the contraction as "real and observable as a property of a material body" — which he noted "was also Einstein's opinion.") The constructive route is not fringe; it is in the canon as a teaching of the same physics.
