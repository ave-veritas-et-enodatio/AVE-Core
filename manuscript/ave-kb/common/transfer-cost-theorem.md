# The Transfer-Cost Theorem — within-SYSTEM exchange is free; the arrow is a boundary crossing into a counted continuum

**Class: consistency / organizing theorem over already-ruled canon.** It originates no
mechanism and predicts no number. What it does is fix the SCOPE at which the corpus's
loss vocabulary is allowed to bite, so that "cost", "loss", "dissipated" and "irreversible"
stop being attachable to any energy movement a sentence happens to find interesting.

## §1 — The statement

> **Within-system reactive exchange is free, however many CHANNELS it crosses. An arrow
> exists iff energy crosses the SYSTEM boundary into a continuum-counted port** — far-field
> radiation, a matter detector, or a topology change. **The cost is not paid to a resistor;
> it is paid in countability.**

Three words carry the whole content and each is load-bearing:

- **SYSTEM, not channel.** The free/costed carve is drawn at the system boundary. Channel
  crossings *inside* the boundary — $L\!\leftrightarrow\!C$, $A_1\!\leftrightarrow\!T_2$,
  electric ↔ magnetic — are free by Axiom 3, indefinitely and at 100% per quarter cycle.
- **CROSSES.** Not "is redistributed". Redistribution across modes or channels of the same
  system is the MODE-loss row of [`retention-transition-split.md`](retention-transition-split.md)
  (`:59`): `requires_R` = **no**, the system conserves.
- **COUNTED.** The port must terminate in something that *counts* — a continuum of external
  modes with reconvergence $\approx 0$, a detector click, or a change in the lattice's own
  mode inventory. A port that returns its energy is a reactive stub, not an arrow.

## §2 — Why the theorem is NOT "there is always a cost"

The literal reading of the origin sentence is **false in AVE and would break Axiom 3.** An
ideal LC tank moves 100% of its energy between the electric and magnetic channels every
quarter cycle, at zero cost, forever — that is the electron's own persistence argument
($Q\to\infty$, [`retention-transition-split.md`](retention-transition-split.md)`:25`,
`:57`). Note that this example **is itself a channel crossing**, which is exactly why the
carve had to move from *channel* to *system*: the counterexample kills both the strong form
(*"transfer costs"*) and the intermediate form (*"channel-crossing costs"*).

What survives is a **scope** theorem, not a cost law: crossing costs **only** at the system
boundary and **only** through a counting port.

## §3 — The three delivery modes

The three delivery modes are the corpus's three ruled loss/irreversibility channels, read in
the delivery register. Canonical enumeration, [`substrate-native-terminology.md`](substrate-native-terminology.md)`:33`,
verbatim: *"the corpus's genuine **loss/irreversibility channels reduce from four to THREE**:
(1) radiative port, (2) boundary-Joule extraction, (3) Regime-IV rupture."*

| # | delivery mode (SVA row-6 name) | ruled loss channel | what does the counting | terminal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **far-field radiation** | radiative port | a continuum of external modes, reconvergence $\approx 0$ | $R_{rad}\equiv Z_0$ — a **delivery** impedance (§4) |
| 2 | **matter detector** | boundary-Joule extraction | the detector's own irreversible record (the click) | $Z_{det}$, a real port at/outside the boundary |
| 3 | **topology change** | Regime-IV rupture | the lattice's **mode inventory itself** changes | no impedance — the canvas, not a port |

Mode 3 is the sharpest counting boundary in the corpus and the only one that is not a port:
what changes is *what modes exist*. The register's own arrow licence — *"the arrow comes from
mode-count or a click, never a valve"* (`research/2026-07-13_f6-tier1-two-reservoir-ledger_CHARTER.md:256`,
verbatim) — is modes 1+3 and mode 2 respectively.

## §4 — $R_{rad}$ is a DELIVERY impedance, not a dissipation (the resolved open edge)

The candidate's original gloss of boundary-Joule extraction as *"counting done by the
detector"* was flagged in the docket as **a reframe, not an instance** (open edge (iv)): the
register books that row with a genuine $\mathrm{Re}(Z)$ — [`retention-transition-split.md`](retention-transition-split.md)`:60`,
verbatim, *"`$R_{rad}\equiv Z_0$` or `$Z_{det}$` (a real port)"*, `requires_R` = **port-only**.

**Ruled reading: a real port at the boundary is a DELIVERY terminal, and delivery is not
dissipation.** The substrate does not heat; the energy leaves, intact, into whatever counts it.
Two merged corpus records carry this:

- The ringdown-wave batch's figure ruling — the `hulse_taylor` caption must read *"radiated
  power **DELIVERED** to the far-field port, not 'dissipated'"*
  ([`2026-08-04-rulings-ringdown-wave-batch.md`](../../../_orchestration/docket-entries/2026-08-04-rulings-ringdown-wave-batch.md) item 3).
- The SVA provenance section names the modification set purchased by *"the GW-memory
  latch-vs-flux and **R_rad delivery** lessons"* ([`standard-vacuum-analysis.md`](standard-vacuum-analysis.md) §2).

So the theorem's *"never paid to a resistor"* clause is precise, not loose: **no resistor
inside the system** takes the payment. A $\mathrm{Re}(Z)$ **at the boundary** is the delivery
port through which the arrow is taken — it is where the system stops, not where the substrate
rubs. **Bench consequence:** no use of "loss", "dissipated" or "Joule" is licensed without
naming which of the three ports the energy was delivered to.

## §5 — The negative control: Op3

**Op3 is the worked case of a channel crossing with NO arrow**, and it is the control that
keeps the theorem from being unfalsifiable-by-construction. $A_1$ empties into the $T_2$
irreps — a genuine crossing between channels — while the code is unitary and *"Conserves
total power"* (`src/ave/core/k4_tlm.py:396-398`). Grant ruled it **LOSSLESS TRANSDUCTION**,
not system dissipation (RULING 21, 2026-07-19; [`retention-transition-split.md`](retention-transition-split.md)`:47`,
[`substrate-native-terminology.md`](substrate-native-terminology.md)`:33`).

Op3 crosses a channel boundary, crosses **no** system boundary, and correspondingly carries
**no** arrow. A theorem that made channel-crossing costly would have to call Op3 a loss; this
one is required to call it free, and canon already does.

## §6 — What this theorem does NOT settle

- **Definition or claim? OPEN.** Whether *"crosses a counting boundary"* **defines** the arrow
  or **claims when arrows occur** — i.e. whether the theorem is a tautology or has content —
  is not settled here. The Op3 control (§5) is the argument that it has content (it forbids a
  reading canon might otherwise have taken); it is not a proof.
- **The near-yield fork is OUT of scope.** Whether the at/above-yield crossing is reversible
  or memristive stays **OPEN** ([`retention-transition-split.md`](retention-transition-split.md)`:61`,
  `:65`, `:67`; Grant leans reversible). This theorem does not bank it either way: if the
  near-yield channel turns out to carry an arrow, the theorem asks which port counted it — it
  does not supply one.
- **Altitude.** Whether this says anything the PRODUCT/TRANSITION split does not already say,
  or is that split re-stated one level up, is the honest null outcome and is not claimed
  against. What is new here is only the **scope word** (system, not channel) and the delivery
  register for $R_{rad}$.
- **It settles no unitarity question.** In particular the black-hole information FLAG
  ([`black-holes-impedance-mismatch.md`](../vol3/cosmology/ch04-generative-cosmology/black-holes-impedance-mismatch.md))
  stays OPEN; the theorem constrains the FORM any answer may take (an arrow must name its
  counting port, and at the horizon no rupture has occurred — the rupture is at $r_{sat}=3.5\,r_s$),
  and picks no answer.
