## §0 — The contract

**What this leaf is.** An **index of established, external theorems** — one canonical framework-neutral statement each, the set of names that theorem wears in the different disciplines the corpus reads from, an **exactness class**, the **ontological-silence ceiling** it carries, its **verified corpus use sites**, and its **regime fence**. It exists because the same theorem arrives in this program under four or five different names, and an agent who meets it under one name does not recognise it under another — so the corpus either re-derives it, or (worse) treats the two names as two independent confirmations.

**What this leaf is NOT, stated once and binding on every consumer:**

1. **It is not a chord list, and cannot be read as one.** Every row here is *established mathematics that predates AVE and belongs to nobody*. A theorem that holds in two frameworks is a statement about the **algebra**, not about the world. This is why the ceiling column is **mandatory on every EXACT row** and why it is not optional prose: the canonical statement of the ceiling is Kron's, verbatim from [`physics-lineage-map.md`](physics-lineage-map.md):359 — ***"an exact isomorphism is ontologically silent."*** T8 records its status as **STANDING**, *"not evaded — conceded and relocated"* (:366). Nothing in this leaf relaxes that.
2. **It mints no physics claim and no id.** No `clm-` / `def-` / `exp-` / `sup-` / `ilk-`; no `confidence`, no `solidity`. Per **INVARIANT-S11** (extend, don't reinvent) and the `clm-eemap1` precedent — a means-tested consolidation of exact-but-silent algebra lands **under** existing structure, not as a new node scheme.
3. **It derives nothing and strengthens nothing.** A row's presence is not evidence for the corpus statement that cites it. If a corpus use site is weak, wrong, or asserted, the row **says so** (see TH-7's asserted-step flags) rather than laundering it.
4. **It is not the AVE-machinery register.** Theorems the AXIOMS force are `clm-`-backed and live in [`foundational-machinery-register.md`](foundational-machinery-register.md) (Theorem 3.1′, the Mass-Closure Theorem, the No-Hair rule, …). This leaf carries **only** theorems the program **imports**. The two sets are disjoint by construction; where they touch, the row cross-refs rather than restates.

**Admission criterion (why the register is short and stays short).** A row lands only if **all four** hold: (a) the object is a **theorem or a named form**, not a phenomenon or an analogy; (b) it wears **≥ 2 genuinely different discipline names** — a thesaurus row for a term with one name has no thesaurus content; (c) it has **≥ 1 verified corpus use site on `main`** (file:line, two-method), and unmerged-branch sites are cited **only** as supplements, always flagged; (d) its **ceiling and regime fence are stateable**. Candidates failing any of the four are recorded in **§5** with the reason, not quietly dropped.

**The five exactness classes:**

- **EXACT-same-theorem** — the names denote the *identical* mathematical statement; only the interpretation of the symbols differs. Proof-level identity, not resemblance. **Always carries a ceiling.**
- **EXACT-same-mechanism-two-tightness-tiers** *(added 2026-08-01 under Grant ruling D3, discharging the TH-3 ★AUDIT-FLAG; one row — TH-3 — is in this class)* — one **proof mechanism**, identically the same argument, evaluated on two structurally different admissible sets, so the names sort into **two labelled tightness tiers that are not interchangeable**: an **outer** tier of scale-free trial-field evaluations (no BVP, no specimen, no boundary layer) and an **inner** tier of size-dependent boundary-condition bounds that converge to the exact property. The *mechanism* identity is exact; the *tightness* is not, and the ordering between tiers is **generically strict**. **Always carries a ceiling** (a shared mechanism is still algebra), and every fence clause on such a row **must name the tier it applies to** — that is the whole point of the class, and it is the discipline the class exists to enforce.
- **structural-analogy** — the same *shape* under a mapping that is not an identity (some load-bearing object fails to map). Not used in §4 this pass; reserved so a future row cannot be silently upgraded.
- **regime-scoped** — an identity that holds *inside* a stated fence and fails outside it. The fence is the content of the row.
- **IMPORTED-FORM** — ★**not a theorem at all**: a constitutive/model form taken from an external literature, carried here *only* so it cannot inherit exactness by sitting next to exact rows. One row (TH-9) is in this class and says so in every cell.

### TH-1 — Dirichlet's principle (minimum potential energy)

- **Canonical statement.** For a positive-semidefinite quadratic energy form on a finite domain, the equilibrium field is the minimiser over all fields matching the prescribed *boundary values*; prescribing more boundary degrees of freedom shrinks the admissible set, so the constrained minimum can only rise.
- **Names across disciplines.** *Potential theory / PDE:* Dirichlet's principle. *Graph theory / numerics:* the Dirichlet energy of a weighted Laplacian, minimised under a Dirichlet partition. *Elasticity:* the principle of minimum potential energy. *Homogenization:* the **KUBC** (kinematic uniform boundary condition) arm — $u = E\cdot x$ prescribed on $\partial V$. *EE-network:* the **voltage-clamped / prescribed-potential** drive — hold the boundary nodes with ideal voltage sources, let the currents float, read the multiport $[Y]$.
- **Exactness class.** **EXACT-same-theorem.** All five are the Dirichlet partition of one PSD quadratic form; the elasticity and network statements differ only in whether the per-edge block is $3\times3$ or $1\times1$.
- **Ceiling.** Kron-class: this is an exact algebraic isomorphism, and *"an exact isomorphism is ontologically silent"* ([`physics-lineage-map.md`](physics-lineage-map.md):359). Recovering it on a lattice is **consistency-class**; it confers no chord and cannot.
- **Corpus use sites.** [`the-sourced-charge-no-go-cascade.md`](the-sourced-charge-no-go-cascade.md):94, :98 — the Dirichlet energy `φᵀ L_w φ = Σ_edge ε_eff·(Δφ)²` on the weighted srs graph Laplacian `L_w = Bᵀ diag(ε_eff) B`, **proof class THEOREM** ("discrete maximum principle / Dirichlet-energy argument"), established three independent ways; mirrored at [`claim-quality.md`](claim-quality.md):1345 (Lock 3). · [`relative-offset-principle.md`](relative-offset-principle.md):57 + [`claim-quality.md`](claim-quality.md):1541 — the `#782` K-side is *"a **KUBC upper bound** … an upper bound cannot support a floor claim"*. · [`translation-tables/translation-circuit.md`](translation-tables/translation-circuit.md):332 — §4.7.2 **row D** (**`#804`, merged 2026-07-30**) · `research/2026-07-28_subc-kubc-bracket_prereg-FROZEN.md`:63 — §1.3's *"**KUBC ⇒ UPPER bound, by the principle of minimum POTENTIAL energy**"* bullet (**`#802`, merged 2026-07-28**). *(Both were off-`main` at draft time and carried the unmerged flag; flags flipped and targets content-verified at HEAD in the 2026-08-02 sweep.)*
- **Regime fence.** Needs a genuinely PSD form. On `main` the use is the **source-free** problem `L_w φ = 0` with **strictly positive** edge weights — the corpus's own caveat, at [`claim-quality.md`](claim-quality.md):1345, is explicit: *"the hypothesis is `S > 0` **strictly**; the `S = 0` (rupture-point, `ε → 0`) puncture is out-of-scope and is lane Z's doorway."* ★**Do not conflate the two on-main readings:** `:94` uses the Dirichlet **energy** to prove a *no-flux* result (a maximum-principle argument); the *bound/minimisation* reading — Dirichlet's **principle** proper — appears only in the bracket lane *(written when that lane was unmerged; **on `main` since `#802`/`#804`, at `2026-07-28_subc-kubc-bracket_prereg-FROZEN.md`:63 and `translation-tables/translation-circuit.md`:332 — the two readings are still two, and the caution below is unchanged)*. ### TH-2 — Thomson's (Kelvin's) principle

- **Canonical statement.** Dual to TH-1: among all flux fields satisfying the balance law and the prescribed sources/injections, the equilibrium flux minimises the dual quadratic form; prescribing the injection pattern shrinks *that* admissible set, so its minimum can only rise.
- **Names across disciplines.** *EE-network:* Thomson's (Kelvin's) principle of **minimum dissipation** — the steady current distribution minimises $\sum_e r_e I_e^2$ over KCL-admissible flows. *Potential theory:* Thomson's principle (the dual of Dirichlet's). *Elasticity:* the principle of **minimum complementary energy**. *Homogenization:* the **SUBC** (static uniform boundary condition) arm — $t = \Sigma\cdot n$ prescribed, no displacement prescribed anywhere. *EE-drive:* the **current-fed** boundary — ideal current sources, potentials float, read $[Z] = [Y]^{+}$.
- **Exactness class.** **EXACT-same-theorem** (the Neumann partition of the same PSD form as TH-1).
- **Ceiling.** Kron-class: the dual partition is an exact algebraic isomorphism no less than the primal one, and *"an exact isomorphism is ontologically silent"* ([`physics-lineage-map.md`](physics-lineage-map.md):359). Duality does not weaken the ceiling; it inherits it unchanged from TH-1.
- **Corpus use sites.** [`electron-plumbing-primer.md`](electron-plumbing-primer.md):159, verbatim — *"the field arranges itself (surface charges) so the **global** steady flow is the one that **minimizes total dissipation** (Thomson's principle)"*. · [`translation-tables/translation-circuit.md`](translation-tables/translation-circuit.md):333 — §4.7.2 **row E** (**`#804`, merged 2026-07-30**) · `research/2026-07-28_subc-kubc-bracket_prereg-FROZEN.md`:64 — §1.3, verbatim: *"Under `t = Σ·n` on `∂V` the equilibrium stress field minimizes the complementary energy over all STATICALLY ADMISSIBLE stress fields … i.e. `SUBC is a rigorous lower (soft) bound on the stiffness`"* (**`#802`, merged 2026-07-28**). *(Both carried the unmerged flag at draft time; flipped 2026-08-02, quote re-verified byte-for-byte at HEAD. The primer anchor is `:159` per `#832` (merged 2026-08-03), which inserted a Step 3.5 block above it; the pre-`#832` anchor was `:101`.)*
- **Regime fence — ★load-bearing, do not drop at cite time.** The on-`main` citation is the **dissipative (resistive)** reading, used as a plumber-physical illustration in a wire, and the primer immediately analogises it to Axiom 3 (:161, *"This is the substrate's own law — Axiom 3"*; ★cite-shift repair 2026-08-03 — read `:103`, shifted by the `#832` Step-3.5 insert, content-verified at the new line). The **analogy is structural** — local greedy rule ⇒ global variational answer — and is **not** a licence to read the substrate as dissipative: the sub-yield interior is **Ax3 lossless-reactive**, so the substrate-side object is **stored** reactive energy, not dissipated power. The algebra is identical only for a **single-sign** form ($g \to b$, one sign of susceptance). For a general AC mixed-reactance network it **fails outright** — see **TH-8**.

### TH-3 — The two-sided effective-property bracket

- **Canonical statement.** One constraint-monotonicity argument, applied twice in dual directions, brackets the exact effective property from above and below — **and it does so at two different tightnesses**, which is the row's content and the reason the names below do not interchange. The full ordering is the **five-member hierarchy** $C^{\text{Reuss}} \le C^{\text{SUBC}} \le C^{*} \le C^{\text{KUBC}} \le C^{\text{Voigt}}$, **generically strict**. The **outer** pair are scale-free uniform-trial-field evaluations; the **inner** pair are size-dependent boundary-value-problem bounds on a *specimen*, and any intermediate boundary condition on that specimen lands between them. Increasing the specimen size tightens the inner pair toward $C^{*}$; it does **not** move the outer pair at all.
- **Names across disciplines — ★TWO LABELLED TIGHTNESS TIERS, not one name-set.** *(The split is the Grant-ruling-D3 content; do not flatten it at cite time.)*
  - **Tier (a) — trial-field / uniform-field OUTER bounds.** *Polycrystal averaging:* the **Voigt** (upper, uniform-strain trial field) and **Reuss** (lower, uniform-stress trial field) bounds. *Composite theory:* the **elementary / first-order** bounds. **Character: scale-free — no BVP, no specimen, no boundary layer, no size dependence.** They are the constraint-monotonicity argument evaluated on a *single assumed uniform field*, so they are computed from the phase moduli and volume fractions alone, and they never converge to $C^{*}$ as anything is grown.
  - **Tier (b) — boundary-condition APPARENT-MODULUS bounds.** *Homogenization / micromechanics:* the **Hill (1963) / Huet (1990) / Hazanov–Huet (1994)** apparent-modulus ordering $C^{\text{SUBC}} \le C^{*} \le C^{\text{KUBC}}$, i.e. the **KUBC / SUBC** pair. *EE-network:* the **Dirichlet–Thomson two-sided bound** on effective conductance, $G^{\text{current-fed}} \le G^{*} \le G^{\text{voltage-clamped}}$, with **Rayleigh's short-cut and cut laws** as the two special cases — ★**qualified, and the qualification is the whole tier discipline in miniature: Rayleigh's laws are not tier-(b) objects in themselves.
