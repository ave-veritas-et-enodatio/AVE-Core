A gravitational wave detector is an impedance antenna. LIGO's 4 km Fabry--Perot arms form resonant cavities in the LC vacuum, where each light bounce amplifies the GW-induced impedance modulation. The passing GW strain $h$ perturbs the local vacuum impedance:

> **[Resultbox]** *GW-Induced Impedance Perturbation*
>
> $$
> \delta Z = Z_0 \cdot h
> $$

> **[2026-06-11 — three-impedance-law channel correction]** A gravitational wave is a **transverse shear wave**, so the impedance it perturbs is the **shear** impedance $Z_{shear} = \rho\,c_{shear}$, not the EM characteristic impedance $Z_0$. The channel-scoped form is $\delta Z_{shear} = Z_{shear}\cdot h$. The $Z_0$ symbol above predates the channel-subscript law; per the three-impedance law $Z_0 \equiv Z_{EM}$ is the **transverse-EM** impedance only (field-symbol registry §3.11; vocab-operator-unification audit §4(b) #2). The LIGO free-space perturbation is unaffected; whether GWs reflect at the saturated wall (where $Z_{shear}$ freezes) is the §4(d) question flagged to Grant.
