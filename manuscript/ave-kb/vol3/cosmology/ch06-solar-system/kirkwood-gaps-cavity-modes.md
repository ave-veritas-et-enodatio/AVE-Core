## Kirkwood Gaps as Cavity Modes

<!-- label: sec:kirkwood -->

The asteroid belt gaps at mean-motion resonances with Jupiter are modelled as cavity modes in the gravitational impedance field: $a_{\text{gap}} = a_J \cdot (q/p)^{2/3}$. All five major gaps (4:1, 3:1, 5:2, 7:3, 2:1) are reproduced to $< 0.3\%$ error.

> **🟡 CONSISTENCY-CLASS note (dated 2026-07-20; ratified-by-merge #741; `consistency-vs-emergence`).** The $(q/p)^{2/3}$ spacing law **reduces to Kepler's third law** applied to a mean-motion resonance: $T_{\text{ast}}/T_J = q/p \Rightarrow (a_{\text{ast}}/a_J)^{3/2} = q/p \Rightarrow a_{\text{ast}} = a_J (q/p)^{2/3}$ — the $2/3$ is the **Kepler exponent, not a lattice number**. The "cavity mode in the gravitational impedance field" is therefore a **relabel of the Newtonian mean-motion resonance**: it fits to $<0.3\%$ *because* it is the Newtonian resonance condition. **Consensus knife, both ways:** AVE adds nothing observable over Newton here (a peer relabel, not a chord); Newton also only fits via the resonance condition — so the two are **peer**, and there is **no AVE-distinct content in the Kirkwood / Oort data.** The only $\ell_{node}$-free AVE-distinct dimensionless numbers the band-map carries ($2/\pi$, $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{10/3}$) live at the **threshold / quantum** scale (~27–35 OOM away), not at orbital frequencies.
