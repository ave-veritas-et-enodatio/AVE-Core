## Symmetric Core Collapse

By running the semiconductor junction model (Section `sec:two_models`) to target the empirical CODATA Nuclear Mass of Silicon-28 ($26053.188$ MeV), the $7\alpha$ solver generates a highly symmetric envelope at inter-alpha distance $R_{\text{bipyramid}} \approx 83.0\,d$ (where $d = 4\hbar/(m_p c) \approx 0.841$ fm, `D_PROTON`)---the single scalar fit per nucleus, with the topology $(Z,A)$-forced, so that the $1/d_{ij}$ summation reproduces the CODATA mass. As a $7\alpha$ closed-shell nucleus, Silicon-28 is an exact-geometric-identity case where $R$ snaps to the unique impedance-matched bipyramid vertex set.

In asymmetric nucleonic shells, the partial valences exhibit massive reactive levers (like the Fluorine halo at $R_{\text{halo}} \approx 398.5\,d$ or even the moderate Aluminum halo at $R_{\text{halo}} \approx 52.6\,d$). When the shell completes perfectly as with the $7\alpha$ Pentagonal Bipyramid, the geometric envelope collapses into a highly stable symmetric structure.

To reproduce the $26053.188$ MeV mass binding the 28 nucleons, the solver coordinates 378 dynamic interconnected $LC$ network elements. The topological rule of Variable-Spacetime Impedance is consistent across the cluster set: closed-integer alpha-cluster sets yield symmetric, high-stability geometries, while incomplete sequences produce asymmetric moments that manifest as reactive electronegativity.
