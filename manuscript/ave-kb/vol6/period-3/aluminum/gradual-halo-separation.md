## The Gradual Halo Separation Effect

The empirical CODATA nuclear mass for Aluminum-27 is $25126.501$ MeV. This mass is the input; the solver fits the single scalar halo distance $R_{halo}$ that reproduces it within the $(Z,A)$-forced $6\alpha + T$ topology (the topology is zero-parameter; the halo distance is the one fitted degree of freedom).

The fit converges to a tolerance below $0.000001\%$ on $R_{halo}$; the Tritium halo bonds to the primary $Z$-axis (North Pole) Alpha centroid of the underlying Octahedron, bounding outward to $R_{halo} = 52.6d$. Aluminum-27 is a core-plus-halo fit nucleus, not one of the closed-shell exact-geometric-identity cases; its end-to-end topological mass ($25132.926$ MeV) agrees with CODATA to $0.02557\%$ (see the macroscopic mass-defect summary).

Compare this bound to Sodium-23. Sodium is built on the $5\alpha$ Bipyramid, which binds the exact same Tritium triangle at $R_{halo} = 50.2d$.

As the scalar capacity of the core increases from $5\alpha$ to $6\alpha$, the bulk matrix repels the halo slightly further. Aluminum's $52.6d$ lever defines a slightly more relaxed, moderately reactive geometry. This structural asymmetry accounts for why post-transition metals show moderate, less aggressive electronegativities than pure Alkali metals.
