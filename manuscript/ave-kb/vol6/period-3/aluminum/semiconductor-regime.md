## Semiconductor Regime Classification

Aluminum-27 ($6\alpha + ^3\text{H}$) is the third core-plus-halo element. The semiconductor engine fixes the Magnesium-24 octahedral core at $R_{\text{core}} = 78.0\,d$ (Small Signal, $V_R/V_{BR} = 0.040$, $M = 1$), then fits the single scalar Tritium halo distance: $R_{\text{halo}} = 52.605\,d$. The $0.000\,000\%$ figure is the fit-convergence tolerance on $R_{\text{halo}}$, not the mass-prediction error---Aluminum-27 is a core-plus-halo fit nucleus (NOT in the closed-shell exact set), and its end-to-end topological mass agrees with the CODATA $25\,126.501$ MeV to the $0.02557\%$ recorded in the summary table.

The Period-3 halo evolution runs $R_{\text{halo}} = 398d$ (Fluorine) $\to$ $50d$ (Sodium) $\to$ $53d$ (Aluminum). As the core grows from $4\alpha$ to $5\alpha$ to $6\alpha$, the halo contracts dramatically and then stabilizes. The Sodium-to-Aluminum shift of only $2.4d$ indicates that the $5\alpha \to 6\alpha$ core expansion has negligible effect on halo proximity---the octahedral core is already dense enough to saturate the core-halo attraction. This geometric plateau explains why post-transition metals (Al, Ga, In) share moderate, similar electronegativities rather than exhibiting the extreme variation seen across Halogens and Alkali metals.
