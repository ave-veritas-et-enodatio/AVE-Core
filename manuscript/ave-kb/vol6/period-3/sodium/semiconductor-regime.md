## Semiconductor Regime Classification

Sodium-23 ($5\alpha + ^3\text{H}$) is the second core-plus-halo element after Fluorine-19. The semiconductor engine fixes the Neon-20 bipyramidal core at $R_{\text{core}} = 81.158\,d$ (Small Signal, $V_R/V_{BR} = 0.032$, $M = 1$), then solves for the single fitted scalar---the Tritium halo distance---that reproduces the CODATA mass of $21\,409.214$ MeV. The result is $R_{\text{halo}} = 50.171\,d$; the $0.000\,000\%$ is the optimizer's convergence tolerance on $R_{\text{halo}}$, not an ab-initio mass-prediction error. (Sodium-23 is a core-plus-halo fit nucleus; its end-to-end mass residual against CODATA is the $0.02436\%$ reported in the macroscopic mass-defect summary.)

The chemical paradox resolved: Sodium and Fluorine share the same structural template ($n\alpha + ^3\text{H}$), yet their $R_{\text{halo}}$ values differ by a factor of $8\times$ ($398d$ vs $50d$). Fluorine's weak $4\alpha$ core repels the halo to an enormous distance, creating a reactive whip. Sodium's dense $5\alpha$ core binds the halo inward to a tight $50d$ lock, producing a compact, rigid bulge. This is why Sodium strips its outer electron easily (low ionization energy) but never aggressively seeks additional bonds (low electronegativity). Within the framework, the alkali-metal energetics map to a single mechanical variable: $R_{\text{halo}}$.
