## §2 — Does occupied-node fraction enter $\xi_{topo}$ / $\mathcal{Q}$? (the occupation-robustness derivation)

### §2.1 — Separate the two factors: the integer $\mathcal{Q}$ and the unit-bridge $\xi_{topo}$

The Cleave prediction $\mathcal{Q} = \xi_{topo}\cdot x$ is a **product of two structurally different objects**, and the occupation question lands differently on each:

| Factor | What it is | Dimensionality | Could occupancy touch it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{Q} = \mathrm{Link}(\partial\Omega, \mathbf{F}_{\text{substrate}})$ | **Gap-protected integer** — boundary linking number, the no-hair charge observable | $\in \mathbb{Z}$, a 1D line/loop count (`boundary-observables-m-q-j.md:20`) | Only via gap-closing (§2.3) |
| $\xi_{topo} = e/\ell_{node}$ | **Frozen-metric unit-bridge** — converts the integer to coulombs, then $V=\mathcal{Q}/C_{in}$ to volts | scalar, C/m (`electromechanical-transduction-constant.md:13`) | Only if $\ell_{node}$ (the Compton metric) changes — it doesn't (§2.4) |

The robustness argument is therefore two independent legs: **(2.3)** the integer is gap-protected against interior occupancy, and **(2.4)** the unit-bridge is a frozen metric untouched by a dielectric in the gap. Both must hold for the floor to lock; both do, with one named caveat.

### §2.2 — What the swept boundary loop actually sees (substrate-native, no-hair observability rule)

The electrometer's floating plate + its guard ring define a closed boundary loop. As the PZT steps the gap by $x$, that loop sweeps through the substrate and the readout is the change in the linking number of $\mathbf{F}_{\text{substrate}}$ through the loop — i.e. $\Delta\mathcal{Q}$. The substrate-observability rule (A-026/A-028 canonical, `boundary_invariants.py:4–11`; `boundary-observables-m-q-j.md:26–31`) states: **for any region $\Omega$ enclosed by a $\Gamma=-1$ saturation surface, only $\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{Q}, \mathcal{J}$ are externally measurable; interior eigenmode wavelengths, microrotation profiles, soliton topology, and bond-stress distributions are invisible** ("the no-hair theorem applied universally … the substrate's fundamental observability constraint at every scale").

Now the solid dielectric in the gap is, microscopically, a packing of **atoms**. An atom is a bounded soliton assembly: nucleus + $Z$ electrons inside an outer-shell saturation boundary, carrying its own $(\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{Q}, \mathcal{J}) = $ (atomic mass, ionization charge, total angular momentum) — this is the **Atom row** of the universal no-hair table (`boundary-observables-m-q-j.md:45`: "Atom ($\ell\sim10^{-10}$ m) | Outer shell saturation | Nucleus + $Z$ electrons | atomic mass, ionization, total angular momentum"). The atom's **Pauli-filled interior** — which shells are occupied, where the $A^2\to1$ saturated nodes sit, the per-node occupancy fraction Grant asked about — is precisely the "interior soliton topology / bond-stress distribution" that the rule declares **invisible** to any external swept loop.

So the swept-loop readout does NOT see the occupied-node fraction. It sees the atom as a no-hair object contributing only its integer $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{atom}}$ (zero for a neutral, intact dielectric atom). **The occupied-node fraction is no-hair-screened from $\mathcal{Q}$.** This is the load-bearing physics, and it is the *same saturation* that defines occupation (a node at $A^2\to1$) which defines the $\Gamma=-1$ boundary (`resonant-lc-solitons.md:42–50`, $\Gamma = (Z_{core}-Z_0)/(Z_{core}+Z_0) = -1$) that makes the interior invisible. Occupation and screening are two faces of one Axiom-4 event — you cannot have the occupancy without the boundary that hides it.

### §2.3 — Why the integer is robust: the Thouless-pump / gap-protection argument

The deeper reason $\Delta\mathcal{Q}$ is an integer robust to interior plumbing is that it is a **topological charge pump**. As the boundary loop sweeps adiabatically through the gapped, filled lattice, the quantity pumped across it is the linking number $\mathrm{Link}(\partial\Omega, \mathbf{F})$ — a topological invariant of the gapped bulk, not an energy extremum and not a function of where the Pauli-filled charges sit inside $\Omega$. The corpus carries the AVE realization of this protection: **topological protection comes from the substrate's impedance gap; a region with no topological protection has $c=0$ (no impedance mirror) and radiates to the trivial ground state, while protected defects (particles) cannot continuously deform out** (`vol2/nuclear-field/ch12-millennium-prizes/poincare-conjecture.md:48,61`; `electron-identification.md:58` "K4 lattice topological protection … the loop cannot untangle"). The integer cannot change by a continuous interior rearrangement (which is what "different occupied-node fraction" is); it can only change by a topology-changing event that closes the gap.

**Therefore: $\mathcal{Q}$ counts the lattice's linking through the swept loop, robust to Pauli-saturated interior occupancy.** Filling the gap with one dielectric vs another rearranges interior occupancy (continuous deformation of the gapped, protected bulk) but cannot change the swept integer — exactly the Thouless-pump robustness the spine invoked.

**The named caveat (gap-protection precondition).** The protection holds *iff the dielectric preserves the substrate's topological gap.* An ordinary insulator does: its atoms are bounded $\Gamma=-1$ assemblies that leave the inter-atomic substrate gap open, so the swept integer is unperturbed. A **gap-closing material** — one that drives the substrate to the rupture/Schwinger boundary $A^2\to1$ *in the bulk between atoms*, $V\to V_{snap}$, where the topological invariant is broken by topology change rather than continuous deformation (`vol1/claim-quality.md:23`: "Topology change above the Schwinger pair-creation threshold … the substrate undergoes a phase transition rather than a continuous deformation, so the topological invariant is broken by topology change, not by violation") — would break the protection and shift the floor. Ordinary bench dielectrics (PTFE, fused silica, vacuum-residual gas) are nowhere near this; they sit deep in Regime I ($S(A)\to1$, the leaf's own classification, `project-cleave-01.md:48`). **So for the bench, the integer is protected and the floor locks; the gap-closing case is a distinct, measurable prediction — §4.**

### §2.4 — Why the unit-bridge is robust: $\ell_{node}$ is the frozen Compton metric

The second leg. Even granting a protected integer, the *volts* read out depend on $\xi_{topo} = e/\ell_{node}$. Could a solid dielectric shift $\ell_{node}$? No: $\ell_{node} = \hbar/(m_e c)$ is the **electron Compton wavelength** (`constants.py:234`, `L_NODE = HBAR/(M_E*C_0)`), the calibration anchor that *sets* the lattice pitch from the observed electron mass — "a UNIT CONVERSION, not a derivation … same status as meter = 1/299792458 of a light-second" (`_archive/L3_electron_soliton/39_alpha_is_calibration.md:145`). It is a property of the **vacuum substrate and the electron**, not of whatever insulator occupies the gap. A PTFE slab does not change the electron's Compton wavelength; therefore it does not change $\xi_{topo}$, the C/m conversion, or the 41.5 mV/μm slope. ($\xi_{topo}$ is dielectric-invariant by construction — it is a frozen-metric constant of $\mathcal{M}_A$, with "**No free parameters introduced**", `electromechanical-transduction-constant.md:25`.)

### §2.5 — Combined verdict: the floor LOCKS

Both legs hold for any ordinary (gap-preserving) dielectric: the integer $\mathcal{Q}$ is gap-protected against interior occupancy (§2.3) and the unit-bridge $\xi_{topo}$ is a frozen metric untouched by the gap material (§2.4). The occupied-node fraction enters **neither** factor. **The 41.5 mV/μm floor is dielectric-invariant — it LOCKS.** The dielectric-invariance leg of the Cleave two-sided discriminator is therefore sound for the bench, with the single, sharp, falsifiable boundary condition that the dielectric not close the substrate's topological gap.

## §3 — Coordinate discipline: this is NOT an A46 phase-space mismatch (phase-space-coordinate-check)

Fired because the derivation touches a topological invariant and a charge-observable. The A46 failure mode is comparing a real-space (lattice-Cartesian) measurement to a phase-space ($V_{inc}, V_{ref}$ / Clifford-torus / $\varphi^2$) corpus claim. **This derivation does not commit that error**, and it is worth stating why so the matrix edit doesn't inherit a coordinate confusion:

- **The corpus claim under test ($\mathcal{Q} = \xi_{topo}\cdot x$) is a dimensional/real-space claim, not a phase-space claim.** $\mathcal{Q}$ is an integer count (a 1D line/loop linking number, `boundary-observables-m-q-j.md:20`) — coordinate-system-agnostic, not an $R/r$ ratio. $\xi_{topo}$ is a scalar C/m unit-bridge — it lives in neither phase-space nor real-space; it is a dimensional conversion. $x$ is a real-space displacement (μm of PZT stroke). The product → volts is a real-space displacement-to-voltage mapping. There is **no $\varphi^2$, no Clifford-torus, no $(V_{inc},V_{ref})$ prediction anywhere in the Cleave chain.**
- **The test measures in matching coordinates.** The electrometer measures a real-space voltage step from a real-space displacement. Corpus claim (dimensional) and test (dimensional voltage) are in the same coordinate system. No transform synthesizes phase-space coordinates from real-space data; no fake-port mapping. Phase-space-coordinate-check **PASSES** — this is the do-NOT-fire case (dimensional/conservation observable where coordinates don't carry a topological $R/r$ invariant).
- **One adjacent corpus fact, correctly kept separate.** The *electron's* topological protection IS a phase-space claim — it lives in the $(2,3)$ Clifford-torus winding, not the $0_1$-unknot real-space body (`vol1/claim-quality.md:23`). That is a *different object* from the charge $\mathcal{Q}$. The gap-protection of $\mathcal{Q}$ I invoke in §2.3 is the **boundary-linking-integer** quantization (the no-hair observability rule on $\mathbf{F}_{\text{substrate}}$), which is consistent with — but not the same as — the electron's internal phase-space-winding protection. ## §4 — The gap-protection caveat is itself a measurable prediction

The robustness in §2.3 is conditional on the dielectric preserving the substrate's topological gap. That condition is not a weakness to hide — it is an **extra AVE-distinct prediction the discriminator gains for free**, and it sharpens the two-sided framing:

- **Ordinary insulators (gap-preserving):** floor LOCKS at 41.5 mV/μm, dielectric-invariant. Swapping PTFE → fused silica → polyethylene → vacuum changes the standard piezoelectric/triboelectric $d_{ij}$ contribution (material-dependent) but leaves the $\xi_{topo}\cdot x$ floor **fixed**. This is the bench discriminator: the AVE floor is the dielectric-invariant residue under material swap (`project-cleave-01.md:42` discriminator, now grounded).
- **Gap-closing / near-rupture media (the new prediction):** a material that drives the inter-atomic substrate toward $A^2\to1$ / $V\to V_{snap}$ in the *bulk* (not just inside its atoms) closes the topological gap; the swept integer is no longer protected by continuous-deformation invariance, and the floor **would shift or collapse**. This is a genuinely AVE-distinct, falsifiable boundary: standard physics has no notion of a "topological-gap-closing dielectric" shifting a displacement-charge floor. It predicts a *threshold* in dielectric stress/field at which the 41.5 mV/μm slope departs.

**Framing note (audit's "frame-on-the-ξ_topo-floor + fixed-$C_{in}$").** The clean way to state the discriminator is: hold $C_{in}$ fixed and read the floor. Under a dielectric swap at fixed $C_{in}$, the *standard* prediction is a material-dependent $Q$ (piezo $d_{31}$ + triboelectric), while AVE predicts the $\xi_{topo}\cdot x$ floor is the **fixed** component and the material-dependent piece rides on top. The two-sided test is then: (P1) is there a non-zero, **gap-independent** floor at all (the polarity-odd, gap-INDEPENDENT component is classically 0.0; the raw vacuum charge is NOT — CPD gives a polarity-odd, gap-DEPENDENT $\propto V_{CPD}/g^2$ term, the dominant Casimir/Kelvin-probe systematic, separated from the floor by the gap-sweep), and (P2) is that floor dielectric-invariant under swap (vs SM's $d_{ij}$-scaling) — with the §4 gap-closing prediction as the sharp upper boundary of the invariance regime.
