## §4 — New question (WALK-LEVEL, not signed): start from the smallest wave?

**Grant (2026-08-14, this session):** *If the electron is the smallest massive
particle, should we start with the smallest wave?*

**Short answer (walk, to be verified):** start with the **linear wave** (the
photon / free \(T_2\)), not with a **smallest** wave. Waves do not have a unique
smallest the way a massive ground state does. The catalog already starts with
the photon; that does not dissolve the \(\ell_{\mathrm{node}}\) posit.

### What "smallest" can mean (three different objects)

1. **Unique massless linear excitation.** The free photon: sub-yield \(T_2\),
   \(Z=Z_0\), \(\Gamma=0\), continuum-Maxwell in the long-wavelength regime
   (`photon-identification.md` line 11 + weak-C 2026-06-15 scope-note).
   Photon and electron are **two amplitude phases of the same underlying
   object** (`photon-identification.md` §4.0). **YES start here** as ontology
   — and the corpus already does. Provenance ("made from a photon") ≠ state
   ("condensed phase of transverse wave content," Grant R1 2026-06-10).
2. **Shortest wavelength the lattice supports.** Brillouin edge
   \(k_{\max}=\pi/\ell_{\mathrm{node}}\). Identifying the electron with that
   edge is the **Nyquist-binding route, CLOSED**
   (`research/2026-06-11_nyquist-binding-route_CLOSED.md`): the electron
   carrier sits a factor \(\pi\) *below* the edge; muon/proton bind *above*
   \(\omega_C\) on the same pitch. **Do not restart this.**
3. **One-cell Compton tank** \(\omega_C=c/\ell_{\mathrm{node}}\). That
   *is* the electron's frequency by the Input-1 identity. Starting there
   "because it is smallest" is circular: the cell was sized from \(m_e\).

A photon has **no unique smallest energy** in the linear continuum (IR is
box-size; UV is Nyquist, which we set from the electron). The ranking
"smallest" is a **mass** ranking. Importing it onto wavelength re-launders
the calibration.
