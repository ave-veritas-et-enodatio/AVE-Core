**The question.** Two neutron stars orbit each other. The pulsar clocks already confirm the
energy they lose matches the *twisting*-wave output alone to 0.16%. Does the vacuum ALSO carry
energy away in its *pressure* channel — and if so, how much?

**Two words, defined once.** A **pressure wave** is a compression pulse — water hammer in a
pipe; sound in a solid's bulk. A **twist wave** is a sideways wiggle that compresses nothing —
shaking a garden hose. In our medium both exist; the observed gravitational wave is canon's
twist wave. A **mirror rating Γ** says what a boundary does to an arriving wave: Γ = −1 is a
perfect mirror that flips the wave's sign; Γ = 0 is no boundary at all.

**The fork.** Canon says every particle in those stars is a tiny pressurized pocket, and the
pocket's skin is a **pressure-only mirror**: Γ = −1 for pressure waves, nearly transparent for
twist waves. So there are two ways the story can go:

- **The leaky branch.** The pocket skins don't matter at orbital frequencies. The stars' whole
  compression content hums at twice the orbital rate, and the pressure channel radiates loud —
  ~1.5–4.5% on top of the twist output. The pulsar clocks forbid that at the 10–30σ level.
  *(This is the branch every kill number to date is computed on.)*
- **The sealed branch.** Think of a speaker driver bolted inside a rigid, sealed box. The
  box's internal reflection comes back sign-flipped and cancels the outgoing sound: a sealed
  box mutes the driver, and the muting is BEST when the note's wavelength is much bigger than
  the box. Here the "note" (twice-orbital frequency) has a wavelength about 10²³ times the
  pocket size — as deep into "sealed-box works" territory as physics gets. If each pocket's
  mirror skin is a true seal, each particle's compression hum cancels itself, and the
  pressure-channel output falls to only the **skin water** — the thin pressurized halo OUTSIDE
  the pocket walls that orbits along with the stars. That residue is much quieter: on the
  sourcing lane's numbers, still ~2.5–40× over the sharpest (double-pulsar) budget, but no
  longer the 10–30σ-class overshoot.

**Why it is unresolved.** The sealed-box cancellation only works if the wall is a true mirror
*at the listening frequency*. The numerical adjudication (#770) established two things: (i)
the canon wall IS buildable in the bond model — the pressure-wave speed inside the skin can be
driven to zero while twist still passes at ~62% of its normal speed (a genuine pressure-only
seal); but (ii) **how hard the physical wall clamps was never derived** — the test rig
hand-set the clamp. Lightly clamped, the pockets leak about half their hum. Heavily clamped,
the leak plateaus near 30% for small groups of pockets and falls as more pockets share the
box. Nobody has derived where the real wall sits on that knob, and the decisive measurement is
not reachable by the quasi-static rigs run so far (#775).

**What is NOT in question.** The twist-wave output (the observed GW) is untouched on every
branch — the wall is pressure-only by canon, and the pulsar data pins the twist rate either
way. Also not in question: the pressure channel exists and is fast (that is settled; PR #913).

**The decision this packet asks for (after the walk).** Does canon FORCE the full clamp — a
true Γ = −1 pressure mirror at every pocket skin, making the sealed branch the physics — or
only a partial one? Sealed ⇒ the standing 10–30σ pressure-channel overshoot collapses to the
skin-water residue (double-pulsar-tested at ~2.5–40×, on numbers whose anchor premise is
itself CONTESTED pending the walk, per R24). Leaky ⇒ the full overshoot stands. The knob to
rule on is the wall depth; everything else in the fork follows from it.
